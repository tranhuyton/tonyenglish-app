  • 1. Study Figure 3.4. Create a food chain with seven levels in it.
  • 2. Suggest why food chains in the Antarctic oceans are more developed than food chains in the Antarctic continent.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Example of a 7-level food chain: Phytoplankton → Zooplankton (Krill) → Small fish → Silverfish → Adelie penguin → Leopard seal → Killer whale (Orca).
  • 2. Food chains in the Antarctic oceans are more developed because the ocean water contains dissolved nutrients, oxygen, and maintains temperatures that support massive phytoplankton blooms. This abundant primary production supports complex food webs. On land, extreme cold, strong winds, lack of liquid water, and poor soil prevent significant plant growth.

Activity 3

  • 1. Describe the relationship between abiotic factors and biotic factors in Antarctica.
  • 2. Explain how Antarctic fur seals are adapted to life in the Southern Ocean.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Abiotic factors (like extreme cold, high winds, and lack of liquid water) dictate the survival of biotic factors (plant and animal life). Biotic factors must heavily adapt to survive these harsh abiotic conditions. Conversely, sunlight availability in summer (abiotic) triggers rapid phytoplankton growth (biotic), supporting the entire ecosystem.
  • 2. Antarctic fur seals have adapted with a thick layer of blubber for insulation, a waterproof coat to keep their skin dry in cold waters, and a streamlined body shape with strong flippers to hunt prey effectively.
