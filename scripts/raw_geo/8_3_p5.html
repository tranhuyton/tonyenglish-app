Activity 2

  1. a) Define environmental sustainability.
    b) What are the main objectives of environmental sustainability?
  2. Explain the concept of the circular economy.
  3. Discuss the difference between social sustainability and economic sustainability.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  1. a) Environmental sustainability centers on the preservation and protection of the natural environment over time.
    b) The main objectives include reducing GHG emissions, transitioning to renewable energy, adopting sustainable agriculture, increasing environmental awareness, and promoting a circular economy to conserve natural resources.
  2. The circular economy is an economic model aimed at keeping raw materials in a closed loop. It focuses on minimizing waste and making the most of resources by repairing, reusing, and recycling, so the waste of today becomes the raw materials of tomorrow.
  3. Social sustainability focuses on the wellbeing of people, tackling inequality, poverty, and ensuring equitable access to resources and opportunities. Economic sustainability involves conducting economic activities in a way that promotes long-term growth and resilience, ensuring that resources are used efficiently and fairly to support the economy without exhausting them.

Activity 3

  1. Explain the benefits of three sustainable techniques used in agriculture.
  2. Comment on the examples given on page 195 to improve sustainability in rock and mineral extraction.
  3. What are sanitary landfills and why are they important?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  1. Sustainable agricultural techniques (like crop rotation, conservation tillage, and organic farming) help preserve soil fertility, reduce soil erosion, minimize water pollution from chemical runoff, and maintain biodiversity, ensuring long-term food security.
  2. Sustainable practices in rock and mineral extraction include: selecting sites near transport hubs to reduce emissions; using advanced methods like water jetting/laser cutting to minimize dust and chemical use; and phased restoration, which returns land to a natural state rapidly, aiding ecosystem recovery.
  3. Sanitary landfills are waste disposal sites designed to isolate waste from the environment until it is safe. They are important because they prevent groundwater contamination (using impermeable liners) and capture harmful greenhouse gases like methane (which can be used for energy), thereby minimizing the environmental and health impacts of waste disposal.

Activity 4

  1. Explain [Incomplete question from source text].
  2. State one reason why most LICs have been keen to accept foreign aid.
  3. What are the two components of debt?
  4. List three characteristics of the Fairtrade system.
  5. What are the advantages and disadvantages of microcredit?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  1. [Incomplete question from source text] - This type of question commonly asks to explain the concept of microcredit or fairtrade in the context of development. For instance, explaining how small loans can empower marginalized individuals to start businesses.
  2. Most LICs have been keen to accept foreign aid because it provides much-needed capital for infrastructure, health, and education projects that the country lacks the domestic resources to fund, helping to accelerate development and alleviate immediate crises.
  3. The two components of debt are: debt outstanding (the total amount of money owed) and debt service (the interest to be paid each year).
  4. Three characteristics of the Fairtrade system include: guaranteeing a fair, minimum price for producers; providing an additional Fairtrade Premium to be invested in community projects; and demanding adherence to certain environmental and labor standards.
  5. Advantages of microcredit include empowering women, encouraging grassroots entrepreneurship, and providing financial services to those excluded from traditional banking. Disadvantages include very high interest rates and the risk that recipients use loans for consumption rather than income generation, leading to debt traps.
