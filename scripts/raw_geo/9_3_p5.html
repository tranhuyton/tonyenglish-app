Activity 9

Figure 9.34 The change in international tourist arrivals, 1990–2024
  • 1. Define tourism.
  • 2. Study Figure 9.34 (page 232). Describe the change in the number of international tourist arrivals between 1990 and 2024.
  • 3. What were the factors responsible for the early development of tourism?
  • 4. Why is an understanding of travel motivators important to travel and tourism companies?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Tourism is the commercial organization and operation of holidays and visits to places of interest.
  • 2. International tourist arrivals generally increased steadily from 1990, interrupted only by economic shocks (like 2008) and severely dropped during the COVID-19 pandemic, followed by a sharp recovery towards 2024.
  • 3. Early development factors included the rise of the railways, increased wealth and leisure time during the Industrial Revolution, and the development of seaside resorts and spa towns for health reasons.
  • 4. Understanding travel motivators allows companies to tailor their marketing and product offerings (e.g., adventure vs. relaxation) to specific target audiences, increasing sales and customer satisfaction.

Activity 10

Figure 9.39 Tourism income and leakages
  • 1. Compare the direct and indirect economic impact of the tourism industry.
  • 2. With reference to Figure 9.39 (page 236), explain the concept of economic leakages.
  • 3. Discuss three economic benefits of tourism.
  • 4. Explain three negative social/cultural aspects of tourism.
  • 5. Comment on Doxey's index of irritation (Table 9.12, page 238).
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Direct impacts are from initial spending in tourism businesses (hotels, airlines, restaurants). Indirect impacts are secondary effects, such as tourism businesses buying supplies from local farmers or paying staff who then spend money in the local economy (the multiplier effect).
  • 2. Economic leakages occur when revenue generated by tourism in a destination (especially LICs/MICs) is lost to outside economies, for example, through profits sent back by foreign-owned hotel chains, or spending on imported goods for tourists.
  • 3. Three benefits: 1) Job creation in the service sector. 2) Foreign exchange earnings helping the balance of payments. 3) Stimulation of infrastructure development (roads, airports) which also benefits locals.
  • 4. 1) Loss of local culture and traditions due to commercialisation. 2) Overcrowding and congestion for local residents. 3) Increase in crime or anti-social behaviour in tourist hotspots.
  • 5. Doxey's index shows how host community attitudes towards tourists change over time from initial 'euphoria' to 'apathy', 'annoyance', and finally 'antagonism' as tourist numbers grow and negative impacts become more pronounced.

Activity 12

  • 1. Explain the importance of tourism to the economy of Jamaica.
  • 2. Describe the location and importance of Jamaica's National and Marine Parks.
  • 3. Describe an example of ecotourism in Jamaica.
  • 4. Define: a) Community tourism b) Pro-poor tourism.
  • 5. Discuss the main problems associated with tourism.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Tourism is a vital part of Jamaica's economy, providing a massive share of its GDP, significant foreign exchange earnings, and direct/indirect employment for hundreds of thousands of people.
  • 2. Jamaica's parks (like the Blue and John Crow Mountains National Park and Montego Bay Marine Park) are located in critical ecological zones. They are important for conserving unique biodiversity, protecting coral reefs, and attracting ecotourists.
  • 3. An example is community-run eco-lodges or guided tours in the Blue Mountains that employ local guides, use renewable energy, and protect local flora and fauna.
  • 4. a) Community tourism: tourism that has close involvement of the local community, where locals manage the industry and retain the profits. b) Pro-poor tourism: tourism that results in increased net benefits for poor people, integrating them into the value chain.
  • 5. Main problems include environmental damage (coral reef destruction, waste generation), economic leakages (profits leaving the country via foreign TNCs), over-reliance on a single vulnerable sector, and social inequality or displacement of local communities.
