Practice Activities

Test your understanding of this topic

Activity 1

  • 1 Why is coal the most important fossil fuel to phase out around the world as quickly as possible?
  • 2 Why are so many countries still reliant on oil as a source of energy?
  • 3 State three advantages and three disadvantages of nuclear power.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1 Coal is the most carbon-intensive fossil fuel and emits the highest levels of CO2 and air pollutants per unit of energy generated.
  • 2 Oil is deeply integrated into global transport infrastructure and petrochemical industries, making it difficult to substitute quickly.
  • 3 Advantages: Low greenhouse gas emissions, high energy density, reliable baseload power. Disadvantages: Risk of catastrophic accidents, radioactive waste disposal issues, high construction costs.

Activity 2

  • 1 Suggest why the location of hydroelectric power is so spatially concentrated both between and within countries.
  • 2 Give two advantages and two disadvantages for each of the following forms of renewable energy: Wind, Solar, Biofuels, Geothermal, Tidal.
  • 3 For the country in which you live, find out which forms of renewable energy are used and how much they contribute to total energy production.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1 HEP requires specific physical conditions: steep relief, narrow valleys for dams, and reliable, high rainfall.
  • 2 Wind: (Adv) Clean, abundant; (Dis) Intermittent, visual impact. Solar: (Adv) Versatile, clean; (Dis) Intermittent, requires large land area. Biofuels: (Adv) Renewable, uses waste; (Dis) Competes with food crops, lower energy output. Geothermal: (Adv) Reliable baseload, low footprint; (Dis) Location-specific, high initial cost. Tidal: (Adv) Predictable, high energy density; (Dis) Disrupts marine ecosystems, expensive.
  • 3 (Student-specific answer based on their country; e.g., in the UK, wind power provides a significant share of renewable energy).

Activity 3

  • 1 How can the efficiency of existing fossil fuel power plants be improved?
  • 2 Explain the meaning of 'energy intensity'.
  • 3 Why are many countries investing heavily in the rollout of smart meters?
  • 4 Why are battery energy storage systems so important in the energy transition?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1 By using combined heat and power (CHP) systems, upgrading turbines, and implementing carbon capture technologies.
  • 2 Energy intensity is a measure of the energy inefficiency of an economy, calculated as units of energy used per unit of GDP.
  • 3 Smart meters provide real-time data to consumers and grid operators, encouraging energy conservation and improving grid management.
  • 4 They store surplus energy from intermittent renewables (solar/wind) and release it during peak demand, stabilizing the grid.

  • 3 Describe Sweden's carbon taxation system.
  • 4 What are the long-term aims of Sweden's energy policy?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1 (Graphing exercise for the student).
  • 2 Sweden has an abundance of fast-flowing rivers suitable for HEP, and vast forest resources that supply biomass.
  • 3 Sweden levies a high tax on carbon emissions, financially incentivizing industries and consumers to switch to low-carbon alternatives.
  • 4 The aims are to achieve 100% renewable electricity production and zero net greenhouse gas emissions by 2045.
