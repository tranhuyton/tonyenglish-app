Activity 1

Figure 5.3 Changes in Arctic sea ice concentration 1850–2013
  • 1. Suggest how the painting The Hunters in the Snow by Pieter Bruegel the Elder and the writings of Charles Dickens suggest change in climate in Europe from the sixteenth century to the mid-nineteenth century to the present.
  • 2. Describe the changes in Arctic sea ice between the periods 1850-1900 and 2000-13 shown in Figure 5.3 (page 111).
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. These historical records describe much colder winters than we typically experience today. Charles Dickens frequently referenced "white Christmases" in his 19th-century writings, while Bruegel's 16th-century painting depicts harsh, extreme winter conditions (often associated with the Little Ice Age). The scarcity of such extreme cold periods today illustrates a warming trend in the climate since the mid-nineteenth century.
  • 2. The extent of Arctic sea ice has decreased significantly. Maps from the later period show a sharp decline in the minimum sea ice cover towards the end of the summer, indicating increased melting and an overall shrinking of ice coverage compared to 1850-1900.

Activity 2

  • 1. Describe what happens to the Earth during:
    a the 100,000-year cycle
    b the 41,000-year cycle.
  • 2. Explain how the eruption of Mount Pinatubo affected global climate.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. a) During the 100,000-year cycle (eccentricity), the shape of the Earth's orbit around the sun changes from more circular to more elliptical, altering the distance from the sun and the amount of solar energy the Earth receives.
    b) During the 41,000-year cycle (obliquity), the tilt of the Earth's axis varies. A greater tilt leads to more extreme seasons, making them longer and more intense.
  • 2. The eruption released massive amounts of volcanic ash and sulfur dioxide into the atmosphere. These aerosols blocked and reflected incoming solar radiation, leading to a temporary decrease in global temperatures.

Activity 3

  • 1. Identify three greenhouse gases.
  • 2. Explain how greenhouse gases affect temperature on Earth.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs). Water vapour is also a common natural greenhouse gas.
  • 2. Greenhouse gases allow short-wave solar radiation to pass through the atmosphere and reach the Earth's surface. However, they absorb and trap a large proportion of the outgoing long-wave radiation (heat) emitted by the Earth. This trapped heat warms the lower atmosphere, a process known as the greenhouse effect.
