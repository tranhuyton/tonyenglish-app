Activity 1

Figure 9.6 Links between economic sectors
  • 1. Define the terms: a) Primary sector b) Secondary sector c) Tertiary sector d) Quaternary sector.
  • 2. Give two examples of jobs in each of the four economic sectors.
  • 3. Describe the food industry product chain shown in Figure 9.6.
  • 4. What job do you want to do when you complete your education? In which sector of employment is this job?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. a) Primary sector: exploits raw materials. b) Secondary sector: manufactures primary materials into finished products. c) Tertiary sector: provides services to people and businesses. d) Quaternary sector: uses high technology to provide information and expertise to all sectors.
  • 2. Primary: farming, mining. Secondary: car manufacturing, food processing. Tertiary: teaching, nursing. Quaternary: research scientist, aerospace engineer.
  • 3. The food industry product chain involves primary sector (farming/fishing), secondary sector (processing and packaging), tertiary sector (transport, marketing, and retail), and quaternary sector (research for improved crops/methods), all connected to deliver food to the consumer.
  • 4. (Student's own answer, e.g., 'I want to be a software developer, which is in the quaternary sector.')

Activity 4

  • 1. Give two examples of locations where companies would be unlikely to be granted planning permission to build a factory or retail park.
  • 2. What are the advantages for some types of manufacturing industry in locating at or close to a large international port?
  • 3. Give two examples of industries where climate might be an important location factor.
  • 4. Why do countries subject to serious natural hazards often find it hard to attract foreign direct investment?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. National Parks and green belts at the edge of urban areas.
  • 2. Break-of-bulk point advantages saving transport costs by avoiding transfer inland; easy receipt of heavy/bulky imported raw materials and easy export of finished goods.
  • 3. Aerospace (needs clear skies for test flights) and the film industry (needs consistent sunny weather, e.g., Hollywood).
  • 4. Natural hazards can destroy infrastructure, factories, and transport links, and pose a threat to worker safety. This increases risks and costs for companies, deterring investment.

Activity 5

  • 1. Which aspects of labour are most important when selecting an area in which to locate?
  • 2. State three factors relating to quality of life that might attract highly skilled labour to a location.
  • 3. Give an example of the way in which technological advance might impact on industrial location.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Cost, quality (skills/education), reputation, turnover rate, mobility, and quantity (availability) of the labour force.
  • 2. Proximity to attractive physical environments, highly rated schools, low crime rates, good quality housing, and high level of accessibility.
  • 3. Advanced mechanisation/automation reduces the need for large pools of unskilled labour, meaning industries might prioritize locating near highly skilled technicians or research hubs rather than low-cost mass labour markets.
