Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Global pattern of LICs, MICs, and HICs:

  • High-Income Countries (HICs): Primarily concentrated in the Northern Hemisphere across North America (USA, Canada), Western and Northern Europe (UK, Germany, France, Norway), and East Asia (Japan, South Korea). Also includes Australasia (Australia, New Zealand) and oil-exporting Middle Eastern nations (Saudi Arabia, UAE).
  • Low-Income Countries (LICs): Heavily clustered in Sub-Saharan Africa (e.g., Chad, Central African Republic, Mozambique, DR Congo, Niger) with isolated examples in Asia (Yemen, Afghanistan, DPR Korea). Most LICs are located in tropical and sub-tropical latitudes.
  • Middle-Income Countries (MICs): Represent the largest grouping, home to 75% of the global population. They dominate Latin America (Brazil, Mexico, Argentina), North Africa (Egypt, Morocco), Eastern Europe, and vast parts of Asia (China, India, Indonesia, Vietnam). Notably, both Asia and Africa now contain more MICs than LICs.

b. Why MICs are making good progress along the development pathway:

  • Rapid industrialisation and manufacturing growth: Transitioning from low-value subsistence agriculture to export-oriented manufacturing (e.g. textiles, electronics, automotive assembly), generating higher wages and foreign exchange.
  • Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) and TNCs: Transnational corporations invest heavily in MICs to take advantage of lower labor and production costs, strategic trade access, and growing domestic consumer markets.
  • Government investment in infrastructure: State revenues and international loans are directed toward major transport links (ports, highways, high-speed rail), reliable electrical power grids, and digital telecommunications.
  • Human capital development: Expanding access to secondary/higher education and vocational training creates a skilled, adaptable workforce capable of high-productivity technical work.
  • Demographic dividend: A high proportion of working-age adults relative to dependants spurs economic productivity and consumer spending.

c. Advantages and disadvantages of using a choropleth map for World Bank classification:

  • Advantages:
    • Provides an immediate visual overview of global and regional income disparities at a glance.
    • Uses standard, recognizable political boundaries and intuitive color shading (darker tones signifying higher income tiers).
    • Facilitates direct comparative spatial analysis between continents and neighboring states.
  • Disadvantages:
    • Hides internal regional variations: Masks severe wealth disparities within large nations (e.g., prosperous coastal megacities like Shanghai vs. impoverished rural interior provinces in China; wealthy São Paulo vs. poor rural northeast Brazil).
    • Visual distortion by land area: Large, sparsely populated countries (e.g., Canada, Russia, Australia) visually dominate the map, whereas small, densely populated, high-income nations (e.g., Singapore, Luxembourg, Bahrain) are barely visible.
    • Artificial boundary effect: Falsely implies sharp, sudden socioeconomic divides right at border lines, whereas real development transitions are gradual across borders.
    • Single economic metric limitation: GNI per capita reflects only average monetary income and ignores crucial non-economic dimensions such as healthcare, education, literacy, inequality (Gini coefficient), and human freedoms.

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Analysis of Proportional Cartograms (both provided for comprehensive reference):

Option 1: Source B (People living in absolute poverty — less than $2.15/day):

  • Description: The map exhibits severe spatial distortion. Sub-Saharan Africa (e.g. Nigeria, DR Congo, Ethiopia, Mozambique) and South Asia (India, Bangladesh) are massively enlarged and swollen, representing the overwhelming majority of global extreme poverty. In stark contrast, North America, Western Europe, Japan, and Australasia are shrunken to thin slivers or vanish completely. Latin America and China appear noticeably reduced relative to their actual physical land area.
  • Explanation: Concentrated poverty in Sub-Saharan Africa and parts of South Asia is driven by structural factors: high dependency ratios, lack of industrial employment, dependence on vulnerable subsistence farming, climatic hazards (recurrent droughts), high national debt burdens, and political instability/conflict. HICs virtually disappear because advanced social security welfare safety nets, minimum wage laws, universal healthcare, and diversified service/industrial economies protect citizens from absolute monetary deprivation.

Option 2: Source C (Underweight children in the world):

  • Description: South Asia is drastically enlarged, with India appearing visually as one of the largest landmasses on Earth. Sub-Saharan Africa is also significantly swollen. In comparison, Europe, North America, Australia, and East Asia (including China and Japan) are shrunk into narrow outlines.
  • Explanation: High rates of child undernutrition in South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa stem from chronic food insecurity, maternal malnutrition, poverty preventing the purchase of nutrient-rich food (proteins, vitamins), and inadequate sanitation and clean water (which leads to repeated diarrhoeal infections that prevent nutrient absorption). In HICs and emerging MICs like China, comprehensive vaccination programs, fortified food supplies, clean piped water, and pediatric healthcare virtually eliminate acute child wasting.

b. Similarities and Surprising Observations:

  • Key Similarities:
    • Both cartograms display acute geographical swelling in the same two macro-regions: Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia.
    • Both maps show high-income regions (North America, Western Europe, Australia, Japan) compressed almost to invisibility, illustrating the tight causal connection between economic poverty and child nutritional deprivation.
  • Surprising Countries / Anomalies:
    • India (The "South Asian Enigma"): India appears even larger and more disproportionately swollen in the underweight children cartogram (Source C) than in the absolute poverty cartogram (Source B). Despite rapid GDP growth and substantial reduction in monetary poverty, child undernutrition remains stubbornly high due to cultural practices, open defecation, and gender disparities affecting maternal nutrition.
    • China: Despite having a population of 1.4 billion people, China appears relatively compact on both maps. This surprises many students, but directly reflects China's massive state-led poverty eradication programs, widespread school lunch programs, and rapid agricultural/industrial transformation over recent decades.
    • Latin America: Remains relatively modest and close to normal geographic proportions on both maps, showing that despite high income inequality (wealth gaps), severe caloric deprivation and absolute poverty ($2.15/day) are significantly lower than in Africa or South Asia.
TASK 3: Reasons for Differences in Development

Summary of Textbook Factors Influencing Development Levels

  • Environmental factors: Climate & droughts, tropical diseases (malaria), landlocked status (9 of 12 lowest HDIs lack sea access), natural hazards (floods, earthquakes), and natural resource endowment (fossil fuels, minerals).
  • Social & Historical factors: Colonial exploitation (draining raw materials to imperial powers), political stability vs. civil conflict, demographic pressures (youth dependency), and access to healthcare and education.
  • Economic factors: World trade imbalances (exporting cheap raw primary goods vs. importing expensive manufactured products), infrastructure quality (transport, power), and ability to attract Foreign Direct Investment (FDI).
  1. a. In pairs, discuss and then agree a rank to show which of the above factors you think will have the most and least influence on the development of a country. Justify your final rank.
  2. b. Explain how political instability can have an influence on national wealth.
  3. c. Norway has a HDI of 0.961 compared with 0.394 for Niger. Which factors will have had an influence on the differences in the HDI scores of these two countries?
💡 View Suggested Answers

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Ranking and Justification of Development Factors (Model Framework):

1. Rank 1 (Most Influential): Political Stability & Governance (Social/Political)

Justification: Governance is the foundational catalyst for all other factors. A transparent, stable government can overcome severe physical disadvantages (e.g. Singapore has no natural resources; Switzerland is landlocked) by investing wisely in infrastructure, the rule of law, and education. Conversely, corrupt or conflict-ridden governments squander vast natural wealth (the "resource curse" seen in DR Congo or Venezuela), deterring investment and stalling progress.

2. Rank 2: Economic Factors & Terms of Trade

Justification: Global trade rules directly control capital accumulation. Countries dependent on raw primary commodity exports (agricultural crops, unprocessed ores) suffer from volatile world market prices and worsening terms of trade against expensive manufactured imports from HICs, trapping them in structural deficits and debt cycles.

3. Rank 3: Social Factors (Education & Healthcare)

Justification: Human capital dictates labor productivity. A healthy, literate, technically educated workforce allows a nation to transition from low-margin primary agriculture to high-value manufacturing and service sectors. Without basic health and schooling, workers remain trapped in subsistence living.

4. Rank 4 (Least Influential in Modern Era): Physical & Environmental Factors

Justification: While landlocked geography, desert climates, and natural hazard vulnerability present genuine obstacles, modern engineering, air/rail freight logistics, and international trade agreements prove that geography is not destiny. Countries with strong institutions and capital can easily mitigate physical constraints.

b. How political instability influences national wealth:

  • Deters Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): TNCs and foreign investors withdraw or avoid entering unstable countries due to severe risks of asset destruction, sudden nationalization, contract cancellation, and hyperinflation.
  • Misallocation of national budget: Government revenues are diverted away from productive developmental sectors (schools, hospitals, road networks, renewable energy) toward military defense, arms procurement, and internal policing.
  • Physical destruction of capital infrastructure: Civil conflict and riots destroy bridges, power plants, telecommunication grids, and factories—costing billions of dollars and decades of development.
  • "Brain Drain" (Human capital flight): Skilled professionals, doctors, engineers, and wealthy entrepreneurs flee the country to safer destinations, depriving the domestic economy of vital skills, leadership, and tax contributions.
  • Economic and trade disruption: Insecurity and damaged supply routes prevent farmers from harvesting or transporting crops, shutter businesses, and reduce national tax revenues to near zero.

c. Explaining the HDI disparity: Norway (0.961) vs. Niger (0.394):

Dimension Norway (Very High Human Development: 0.961) Niger (Low Human Development: 0.394)
Economy & Income (GNI/capita) High GNI per capita ($82,000+). Prudently invested North Sea oil wealth into the world's largest Sovereign Wealth Fund (> $1.5 trillion), providing long-term economic stability and state funding. Low GNI per capita (~$1,300). Economy dominated by rainfed subsistence agriculture and volatile raw uranium exports, generating negligible domestic tax revenue.
Health & Life Expectancy Life expectancy ~83.2 years. Universal free healthcare, advanced medical technology, high doctor ratios, clean piped water, and universal sanitation. Life expectancy ~62.8 years. Extreme shortage of doctors, high infant mortality, endemic infectious diseases (malaria), and chronic malnutrition exacerbated by droughts.
Education & Literacy Mean years of schooling: 13.9 years; adult literacy > 99%. Free universal schooling through university level; high gender equity in education and workforce. Mean years of schooling: 2.1 years; adult literacy ~35%. Severe shortage of rural schools/teachers; high fertility rate (> 6.7) and cultural barriers restrict female schooling.
Physical Geography & Governance Sheltered, ice-free deepwater coastline allowing effortless maritime global trade. Decades of stable democratic governance, low corruption, and strong rule of law. Completely landlocked in the hyper-arid Sahel region, suffering from desertification and extreme transport transit costs. History of political coups and regional insurgencies.
