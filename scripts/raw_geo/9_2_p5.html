Activity 6

  • 1. Define globalisation.
  • 2. What is a global city?
  • 3. Explain the new international division of labour (NIDL).
  • 4. What is foreign direct investment?
  • 5. Discuss three factors (other than those covered in the previous activities) encouraging the globalisation of economic activity.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Globalisation is the rapidly increasing and more complex global links (economic, cultural, and political) that have developed since the 1960s.
  • 2. A global city is a major financial and decision-making centre that acts as a command centre of the global economy (e.g., New York, London, Tokyo).
  • 3. The NIDL divides production into different skills and tasks that are often spread across a number of countries to optimize costs and efficiency.
  • 4. Foreign direct investment (FDI) is investment made by a firm or individual in one country into business interests located in another country, often through TNCs.
  • 5. 1) Trade liberalisation through organizations like the WTO reducing tariffs and quotas. 2) The opening up of economies like China and India. 3) Deregulation of world financial markets allowing greater international competition.

Activity 8

Figure 9.27 Model of TNC development over time
Figure 9.28 Impacts of TNC investment on host developing countries
  • 1. Define a transnational corporation.
  • 2. Why have TNCs been so important in the process of globalisation?
  • 3. What are global value chains and how are they related to TNCs?
  • 4. Study Figure 9.27 (page 227). What are the locational changes often characteristic of the development of TNCs?
  • 5. Look at Figure 9.28 (page 227). For developing countries, discuss two possible negative effects and two possible positive effects of hosting a TNC.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. A transnational corporation (TNC) is a large enterprise that operates in at least one other country besides the country in which it is headquartered.
  • 2. TNCs drive global trade, foreign direct investment, and the spread of technology and skills. Their intra-firm trade and global supply chains tie the global economy together.
  • 3. Global value chains (GVCs) refer to the process where different stages of production are located across multiple countries to add value at each stage at the lowest cost. TNCs are the primary architects of GVCs.
  • 4. TNCs often start with activities concentrated in their home country, then establish branch plants or subsidiaries abroad (often for market access or cheaper labour), and eventually decentralize research and development to various global regions.
  • 5. Positive effects: Provision of investment and infrastructure; creation of jobs and training. Negative effects: Economic leakages (profits returning to the home country); potential environmental exploitation or poor working conditions.
