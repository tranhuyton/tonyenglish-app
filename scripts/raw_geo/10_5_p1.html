10.5 Global Patterns of Energy Supply and Demand

A macro-geographical analysis of international energy flows: the continuous surge in global primary consumption; regional fuel mix divergences across the seven continents; the geopolitical anatomy of energy security; and the 3-stage systemic vulnerability of supply interruptions.

1

Global Consumption Surges & Evolving Primary Fuel Shares

Between 2000 and 2023, total global primary energy consumption expanded by over 50%, driven by rapid industrial expansion, urbanization, and consumer motorization across emerging economies in the Asia-Pacific region.

Figure 10.28: Total Primary Energy Growth

Global primary energy consumption rose from ~390 exajoules in 2000 to over 590 exajoules in 2022. Fossil fuels continue to supply over 80% of total energy.

Figure 10.29: Shifting Energy Fuel Shares

Oil remains the single largest fuel (~31%), followed by coal (~27%) and natural gas (~24%). Modern renewables (wind and solar) have surged exponentially, reaching ~7.5%.

Energy Security & The 3-Stage Interruption Sequence (Fig 10.31)

Energy Security is defined as the uninterrupted availability of energy sources at an affordable price. Countries with domestic energy surpluses (e.g. Canada, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Norway) enjoy high energy security. In contrast, nations that import over 70% of their fuel (e.g. Japan, Germany, Singapore) remain acutely vulnerable to geopolitical choke-points and supply disruptions.

Figure 10.31: The three-stage chronological sequence of an energy supply interruption (Hodder Education).
STAGE 1

The Interruption Trigger

The initiating catalyst: armed conflict, military blockade of maritime choke-points (Strait of Hormuz, Malacca), natural disaster destroying refinery terminals, pipeline sabotage, or cyberattacks on smart electrical grids.

STAGE 2

Direct Physical Impacts

Immediate operational paralysis: rolling electrical blackouts, petrol queues at filling stations, emergency rationing for essential hospital transport, and temporary factory shutdowns.

STAGE 3

Socio-Economic Repercussions

Long-term macroeconomic damage: soaring inflation, rising food production costs (diesel pumps and nitrogen fertilisers), industrial bankruptcies, and public protests.

🎓 Cambridge IGCSE Exam Strategy: Global Fuel Mix & Energy Security
In exam answers assessing energy security risks, always cite specific geopolitical case studies: evaluate how oil-importing economies insulate themselves by establishing strategic petroleum reserves, constructing liquefied natural gas regasification terminals, and aggressively expanding domestic renewable wind and nuclear capacity.
