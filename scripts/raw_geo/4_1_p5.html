Practice Activities

Test your understanding of this topic

Activity 1

  • 1. Compare the oceanic crust with the continental crust.
  • 2. Compare the inner core with the outer core.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Oceanic crust is mostly basalt, denser, and thinner. Continental crust is mostly granite, less dense, and thicker. Oceanic crust subducts beneath continental crust when they collide.
  • 2. The inner core is solid and extremely dense (five times denser than surface rocks), while the outer core is semi-molten/liquid.

Activity 2

  • 1. Explain the difference between slab pull and ridge push.
  • 2. Identify one oceanic plate, one continental plate and one small plate.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Ridge push occurs at mid-ocean ridges where magma rises and gravity pushes the new plate downwards and apart. Slab pull occurs at subduction zones where the denser sinking oceanic plate pulls the rest of the plate behind it.
  • 2. Oceanic: Pacific Plate. Continental: Eurasian Plate. Small plate: Juan de Fuca Plate, Caribbean Plate, Arabian Plate, or Iranian Plate.

Activity 4

Figure 4.6 Global distribution of volcanoes and earthquakes
  • 1. Describe the global distribution of earthquakes as shown on Figure 4.6.
  • 2. Explain why some volcanoes occur in locations away from plate boundaries.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Earthquakes are unevenly distributed and mostly occur in narrow bands along tectonic plate boundaries, such as the Ring of Fire around the Pacific Ocean.
  • 2. Some volcanoes occur away from plate boundaries at hotspots, where plumes of hot magma rise from the mantle and melt through the crust (e.g., Hawaii).
