Activities (Block 1)

  • 1. Evaluate two strategies used to manage urban growth.
  • 2. Compare the strategies to manage either housing or transport in urban areas in LICs and HICs.

Activities (Block 2)

  • 3. Compare the challenges and opportunities brought about by urban growth in Shanghai.
  • 4. Assess the strengths and weaknesses of strategies to manage urban growth.
💡 View Suggested Answers

Activities (Block 1) Answers

  • 1. Green belts: Highly effective at limiting outward urban sprawl and preserving countryside, but can drive up urban land prices and cause 'leap-frog' development. Urban renewal (e.g. gentrification): Revitalizes derelict inner-city areas and uses existing infrastructure, but can price out poorer original residents, increasing social inequality.
  • 2. Housing: In HICs, strategies focus on large-scale urban regeneration, building high-density affordable housing, and decentralizing to new towns. In LICs, strategies often involve upgrading existing informal settlements (e.g., site-and-service schemes or the Favela Bairro project) to provide legal tenure and basic utilities since governments cannot afford to build enough formal housing.

Activities (Block 2) Answers

  • 3. Challenges: Extreme overcrowding, high water stress, severe pollution (water and air), massive waste generation, and acute housing shortages leading to the displacement of lower-income residents. Opportunities: Rapid economic expansion transforming the city into a global financial centre and major port, huge investments in public transport (Metro and expressways), and diversified employment opportunities in service and high-tech sectors.
  • 4. Strengths: Strategic top-down management (like Shanghai's Master Plan) can rapidly deliver world-class infrastructure, efficiently manage huge population shifts through satellite towns, and generate enormous economic wealth. Weaknesses: Such strategies often prioritize economic growth over social equity, leading to wide wealth disparities. Forced relocations can disrupt communities, and aggressive expansion can cause significant environmental degradation.
