TASK 1: Study Source A

Study Source A: The main characteristics of an earthquake

Source A: The main characteristics of an earthquake
  1. Explain the difference between the focus and the epicentre of an earthquake.
  2. Explain why an earthquake would be felt the most strongly at the epicentre.
  3. In pairs, discuss why an earthquake can be felt at great distances from the epicentre. Write two bullet points supporting your answer.
  4. Do you think that the depth of the earthquake below the surface would affect the strength of the earthquake at the surface? Discuss this in pairs and write down a brief answer giving your reasons. Then, share your ideas with the class.

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Difference between the focus and the epicentre:

  • The focus (or hypocentre) is the exact point underground within the Earth's crust where rocks fracture or slip, and seismic energy is first released.
  • The epicentre is the location on the Earth's surface directly vertically above the focus, where seismic shockwaves first reach the surface.

b. Why an earthquake is felt most strongly at the epicentre:

  • The epicentre is the point on the surface closest to the focus.
  • As seismic waves travel outwards through rock, they lose energy over distance due to friction, absorption, and dispersion (attenuation). At the epicentre, seismic waves have traveled the shortest distance through the crust and retain their greatest energy and highest amplitude, causing the most violent ground shaking.

c. Why an earthquake can be felt at great distances from the epicentre:

  • Enormous energy release: High-magnitude earthquakes release massive amounts of energy that propagate outward through dense bedrock as body waves (P-waves and S-waves) and along the surface as surface waves for hundreds or thousands of kilometres before fully dissipating.
  • Geological conditions and wave amplification: When seismic shockwaves pass from dense bedrock into softer sedimentary rocks, loose sands, or deep alluvial basins, their speed slows down but their amplitude increases significantly, amplifying ground shaking at great distances.

d. Effect of focal depth on surface strength:

  • Yes, focal depth significantly affects the strength and destructiveness of shaking at the surface.
  • Shallow-focus earthquakes (0–70 km deep): Cause far more severe shaking and catastrophic damage because the focus is very close to the surface, meaning shockwaves lose very little energy before striking infrastructure.
  • Deep-focus earthquakes (over 300 km deep): Dissipate much of their energy traveling through hundreds of kilometres of mantle and crust, resulting in significantly attenuated and weaker surface ground motion.

Source B: Visible ground displacement across a road, caused by an earthquake
  1. Describe the movement of the land that caused this type of damage during an earthquake.
  2. If the soil had a high water content during this earthquake, what other hazard would you expect to see? How could this hazard present a danger to the people in the affected area?

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Movement of the land causing this damage:

  • The photograph illustrates ground displacement and surface fault rupture.
  • The blocks of land on opposing sides of the active fault line moved relative to each other: laterally (horizontal shear / strike-slip faulting) and/or vertically (differential uplift or subsidence).
  • This sudden ground rupture sheared and ripped the asphalt roadway apart, misaligning the road and crushing/buckling the steel guardrails.

b. Hazard with high water content & dangers to people:

  • Expected Hazard: Soil Liquefaction (as well as mudflows or landslides if on sloping terrain).
  • Dangers to people and infrastructure:
    • Water-saturated unconsolidated sand or silt loses its shear strength under strong cyclical shaking, behaving temporarily like a dense liquid or quicksand.
    • Building foundations lose their bearing support, causing multi-story buildings, bridges, and electric poles to tilt, sink, or collapse.
    • Buried utility lifelines (gas, potable water, sewage pipes) fracture, causing fires, contaminated water supplies, and urban flooding.
    • Roads become cracked, buckled, or submerged in slurry, severing transportation and blocking emergency response and evacuation vehicles.
