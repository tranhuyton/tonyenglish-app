5.3 Responses to Climate Change: Mitigation & Adaptation

A rigorous critical evaluation of global and localized climate solutions: contrasting decarbonisation and carbon sequestration (mitigation) against risk management and resilience building (adaptation), with an in-depth case study of the Bengal Delta.

1

The Golden Rule & Global Climate Governance

Nghe cả phần

Humanity's strategic framework for confronting climate change operates across two distinct but interdependent pathways. In IGCSE examinations, you must never conflate them:

Tackling Causes

Mitigation (Giảm Thiểu)

Action taken to prevent, reduce, or slow down greenhouse gas emissions, or to enhance biological and technological carbon sinks to remove CO₂ from the atmosphere.

Core Examples: Renewable energy (solar, wind, HEP), Carbon Capture & Storage (CCS), afforestation, electric mobility, energy-efficiency retrofits.
Managing Symptoms

Adaptation (Thích Ứng)

Action taken to adjust ecological, social, and economic systems to cope with the unavoidable risks and impacts of ongoing and future climate change.

Core Examples: Coastal sea walls and embankments, elevated stilted cyclone shelters, drought/salt-tolerant crops, floating gardens, early warning radar.
The Evaluation Paradox: "The less mitigation society achieves today, the more it will be forced to adapt in the future." Adaptation is often cheaper and faster to implement locally in the short term, but becomes progressively unviable if planetary warming exceeds 2.0°C–3.0°C. Mitigation is essential for long-term planetary survival, but suffers from high upfront capital costs, long technological gestation periods, and international political friction.

Evolution of Global Climate Accords

Nghe phần này
1997

Kyoto Protocol

Set legally binding emission reduction targets (average 5%) for developed nations. Hampered when the USA refused to ratify, and Canada and Russia subsequently withdrew.

2015

Paris Agreement (COP21)

Ratified by 196 nations. Legally committed to hold warming well below 2.0°C and pursue efforts toward 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels, targeting Net Zero by 2050 via Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs).

2023

COP28 Dubai Consensus

Included historical language calling on all nations to "transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems" and triple global renewable power capacity by 2030.

Key Examination Insights: The steep upward trajectory since 1980 confirms that voluntary international treaties prior to Paris were largely ineffective at curbing atmospheric thermal momentum. Overcoming international "free-rider" problems requires enforceable carbon border taxes and robust international climate finance transfers from MEDCs to LEDCs.

Detailed Named Case Study: Bangladesh (Bengal Delta)

Nghe cả phần
Core IGCSE Concept: Climate Injustice

Bangladesh contributes less than 1% (<0.5%) of total global greenhouse gas emissions, yet it ranks consistently among the top 10 most severely climate-impacted nations worldwide. Between 2000 and 2019, Bangladesh suffered US$3.7 billion in economic losses across 185 extreme weather events.

Figure 5.13: Flood risk distribution across Bangladesh: flash flood, river flood, and tidal/cyclone surge flood zones (Hodder Education).

Topographical Vulnerability: Two-thirds of Bangladesh's land area lies less than 5 meters above sea level, and over 50% of the population (~90 million people) have "high" exposure to regular flooding. A 50 cm sea level rise will submerge 11% of the country, directly displacing 18 million people. A 2-meter rise will displace 50 million climate refugees.

Bangladesh Climate Action Architecture: BCCSAP & NDCs

Nghe phần này

The Bangladesh Climate Change Strategy and Action Plan (BCCSAP) established a world-first dual blueprint containing five adaptation pillars and one mitigation pillar. Under its updated 2021 Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs):

Unconditional Domestic Target
6.75% (>27 Mt CO₂e)
Emissions reduction achieved exclusively utilizing Bangladesh's own internal budget by 2030.
Conditional Target
15.0% (>60 Mt CO₂e)
Additional decarbonisation contingent upon international technological and financial support.
Combined NDC Pledge
21.75% (~90 Mt CO₂e)
Total national greenhouse reduction commitment across industrial, power, and transport sectors.
