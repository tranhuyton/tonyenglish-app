  • 3. Study Table 6.6. Discuss the influence on fertility of one factor from each of the four categories.

💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. The total fertility rate is the average number of children born to a woman during her lifetime.
  • 2. Africa has the highest rate (4.3). Asia and Latin America are tied at 1.9. Oceania is at replacement level (2.1). North America (1.6) and Europe (1.4) have the lowest rates.
  • 3. Demographic: Older female populations have lower fertility. Social/cultural: Higher female education leads to lower fertility. Economic: High costs of raising children in urban areas discourage large families. Political: Anti-natalist population policies strongly dictate lower birth rates.
  • 4. Global fertility decreased from an average of five births per woman in 1950 to 2.2 births in 2023. Also, 103 countries have fallen below replacement-level fertility.

Activity 4

  • 1. Explain the difference between pro-natalist and anti-natalist policies.
  • 2. How has France tried to encourage a higher birth rate?
  • 3. Why did China change from an anti-natalist policy to a pro-natalist policy?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Pro-natalist policies aim to encourage higher birth rates, while anti-natalist policies aim to discourage large families and lower birth rates.
  • 2. France provided longer maternity and paternity leave, higher child benefits, and preferential treatment for government housing.
  • 3. China changed policies to address demographic ageing, an unbalanced sex ratio, a generation of "spoiled" only children, and a shrinking future workforce.

Activity 5

  • 1. What is a geographical model (such as the demographic transition model)?
  • 2. Explain the reasons for falling mortality in stage 2 of demographic transition.
  • 3. Why does it take some time before the fall in fertility follows the fall in mortality?
  • 4. What are the characteristics of stage 5?
  • 5. Discuss the merits and limitations of the demographic transition model.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. A geographical model is a simplification of reality that helps explain and understand the most important aspects of a process over time or space.
  • 2. Mortality falls in stage 2 due to better nutrition, improved public health (clean water, sewage systems), and medical advances.
  • 3. Social norms and cultural expectations surrounding large families take time to adapt to lower mortality rates.
  • 4. Stage 5 features natural decrease, where the birth rate falls below the death rate, leading to population decline.
  • 5. Merits: It provides a simple framework to understand the general transition of countries' populations over time. Limitations: It is highly Eurocentric, fails to account for migration, and many developing countries undergo much faster transitions than the model predicts.
