Practice Activities

Test your understanding of this topic

Activity 1

  • 1. Explain why the emperor penguin is vulnerable to global climate change.
  • 2. Explain why plastics and fishing equipment are a threat to Antarctica's ecosystems.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Emperor penguins are highly vulnerable because they depend completely on stable sea ice for breeding and raising chicks. Premature breaking of sea ice due to warming leads to breeding failures, and a decline in sea ice also reduces krill populations, which is their main food source.
  • 2. Plastics and fishing equipment pose entanglement risks for marine life such as seals and seabirds, leading to severe injury or death. Additionally, degraded microplastics are ingested by organisms like krill, causing internal blockage and bioaccumulating up the food web.

Activity 2

  • 1. Outline the impacts of global climate change on Antarctica's ecosystems.
  • 2. Explain how changes in the whaling industry led to ecosystem destruction on Signy Island.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Impacts include rising temperatures causing ice shelf collapse, shrinking habitats for ice-dependent species, the introduction and spread of non-native species due to milder conditions, and shifts in the marine food web stemming from krill population declines.
  • 2. The whaling industry led to the near-extinction of baleen whales. Processing the whales onshore dumped massive quantities of organic waste (blood, offal) into the coastal waters of Signy Island, artificially altering the local food web and increasing populations of scavenging species, thereby disrupting the natural ecosystem balance.

Activity 3

  • 1. Outline the main principles and objectives of the Antarctic Treaty.
  • 2. Compare the potential impacts of low- and high-global warming emissions on Antarctica.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. The main principles include ensuring Antarctica is used only for peaceful purposes, promoting international scientific cooperation and information sharing, and banning nuclear explosions and radioactive waste disposal.
  • 2. Low emissions would result in a slower temperature rise, allowing ecosystems some time to adapt and limiting the overall loss of sea ice. High emissions would lead to rapid and drastic temperature increases, severe reduction of sea ice, massive ice shelf collapse, and the potential collapse of ice-dependent ecosystems.

Activity 4

  • 1. Define the term 'environmental impact survey'.
  • 2. Outline the potential impacts of new buildings on Antarctica's environment.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. An 'environmental impact survey' is a detailed study conducted before a development begins to identify, evaluate, and mitigate the potential negative effects the project might have on local ecosystems and conservation areas.
  • 2. New buildings can cause significant soil and permafrost disturbance, vegetation trampling during construction, disruption of wildlife breeding sites, pollution risks, and the accidental introduction of alien species.
