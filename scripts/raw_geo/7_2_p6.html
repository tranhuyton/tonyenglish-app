Source A: Opportunities of urban living

Table 7.3: The opportunities of urban living

HousingCities often provide a greater variety of housing (quality and quantity); therefore, a range of different income groups can live within cities.
Economic developmentMore jobs, a centralised market, and faster trade lead to increased production and services in high-density urban areas. Cities are often the focus of finance and retail.
Increased consumptionGenerally higher individual wealth means people living and working in large towns and cities have more disposable income to spend on a wider choice of goods and services.
EmploymentThere are more jobs and a greater variety of jobs in urban areas. This means there are low-skilled and high-skilled jobs available in both the formal and informal economies.
ServicesGenerally, access to health and educational services are significantly better in cities. Hospitals are often more accessible. Large towns and cities often have better access to electricity, piped water, safe drinking water, and internet access.
Leisure & entertainmentMost amenities and entertainment are easy to reach. Clubs, restaurants, and cinemas are more commonly found in cities.
CultureCities and towns tend to have more cultural and ethnic diversity, which is reflected in a greater mix of restaurants, cultural institutions, and entertainment venues.
  1. a. Source A shows four images of cities. In pairs, discuss the potential opportunities of urban living shown in the photographs. How could you classify these opportunities?
  2. b. Sometimes the opportunities of working and living in urban areas can cause 'conflicts' between different stakeholders (groups of people who work and/or live in cities). Explain two conflicts that may be associated with groups of people who work and live in cities.

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Potential opportunities and classification:

  • Opportunities observed: High-density modern commercial and financial districts (London - Gherkin); vibrant 24/7 retail, dining, and nightlife economies (Tokyo street); accessible public transit systems (Istanbul tram); rich cultural diversity, carnivals, and public festivals.
  • Classification:
    • Economic opportunities: Greater variety and quantity of high-skilled professional jobs, higher wages, retail commerce, and commercial investment.
    • Social opportunities: Access to specialized public services, hospitals, higher education institutions, and reliable mass transit.
    • Cultural & Leisure opportunities: Diverse entertainment venues, international cuisines, multicultural celebrations, and social integration.

b. Two conflicts between stakeholders:

  • Conflict 1: Property Developers vs. Low-Income Local Residents (Gentrification): Private developers construct luxury high-rises or commercial complexes for maximum profit, which drives up local property values, rents, and municipal taxes, forcing long-standing low-income residents out of their neighbourhoods.
  • Conflict 2: Motorists/Commuters vs. Pedestrians/City Planners (Urban Space Allocation): City planners and green advocates want to pedestrianize streets, add dedicated cycle lanes, and restrict traffic to combat air pollution and noise. This frustrates private motorists, taxi drivers, and delivery businesses who face increased congestion, slower travel times, and scarce, expensive parking.

  1. a. With reference to Sources B and C, explain one economic, one social, and one environmental problem created by traffic congestion in cities.
  2. b. Explain why urban inequalities often increase as cities rapidly grow.
  3. c. Suggest two advantages and two disadvantages of working in the informal economy.
  4. d. Many people move to urban areas in LICs for a better quality of life. Do you think all people achieve this? Give reasons for your answer.
💡 View Suggested Answers

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Problems created by traffic congestion:

  • Economic problem: Massive loss of productive working hours as employees sit stuck in gridlock; delayed delivery of commercial freight and perishable goods; wasted fuel expenditure running idle engines.
  • Social problem: Elevated chronic stress, fatigue, and road rage; increased respiratory illnesses (asthma, bronchitis) and cardiovascular issues from breathing concentrated toxic vehicle exhaust; loss of leisure and family time.
  • Environmental problem: Substantial emission of greenhouse gases (CO2) driving climate change, plus local atmospheric air pollutants (NOx, particulate matter PM2.5, carbon monoxide) forming toxic photochemical smog (as visible in Los Angeles, Source C).

b. Why urban inequalities increase during rapid growth:

  • Rapid migration outpaces municipal capacity to build affordable housing, schools, healthcare clinics, and paved roads.
  • A small educated elite and business owners capture the high-paying formal economy opportunities and live in secure, affluent districts. Meanwhile, millions of unskilled migrants are forced into overcrowded informal squatter settlements (favelas/slums) lacking clean piped water, electricity, or sanitation.

c. Advantages and disadvantages of working in the informal economy:

  • Advantages:
    • Immediate source of livelihood for poor or unskilled workers with no barriers to entry (requires no formal education or legal permits).
    • Work hours and operations are flexible, and workers pay no income tax.
  • Disadvantages:
    • No job security, employment contracts, minimum wage, or legal protection against exploitation.
    • No social safety nets (no sick leave, health insurance, unemployment benefits, or pension).

d. Do all people moving to urban areas in LICs achieve a better quality of life?

  • No, many migrants fail to achieve a higher quality of life.
  • While some find stable work and better schooling for children, millions end up trapped in squalid informal settlements (shanty towns) with worse health risks than rural areas, exposed to contaminated water, open sewage, landslides, high crime rates, and insecure informal labor.

  2. b. Study the cartoon in Source D. What do you think the cartoon is suggesting about urban sprawl?
  3. c. Describe and explain one social, one economic, and one environmental impact of urban sprawl on the rural-urban fringe.
💡 View Suggested Answers

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Definition of 'urban sprawl':

  • Urban sprawl is the rapid, often uncontrolled or unplanned outward expansion of a city's built-up physical boundary into the surrounding countryside, open green spaces, or the rural-urban fringe.

b. Meaning of the cartoon in Source D:

  • Encroachment on greenfield farmland: Urban sprawl is depicted as an unstoppable, towering tidal wave of concrete skyscrapers and construction labeled "URBAN SPRAWL" bearing down on a serene farm ("Old MacDonald had a farm").
  • Commercial pressure on farmers: The farmer is shaking hands with a suit-wearing developer holding a property deed next to a "FOR SALE" sign, illustrating that farmers face immense financial temptations or pressures to sell productive agricultural land to commercial property developers.
  • Loss of rural heritage: The cartoon contrasts the timeless innocence of pastoral farming with the aggressive, disruptive nature of modern suburbanisation.

c. Impacts of urban sprawl on the rural-urban fringe:

  • Social Impact:
    • Impact: Disruption of traditional village community life and increased pressure on local amenities.
    • Explanation: The arrival of urban commuters with different lifestyle patterns alters the social fabric of rural villages. Local schools, GP surgeries, and roads become congested as population density surges.
  • Economic Impact:
    • Impact: Decline of inner-city businesses and high infrastructural costs for municipal councils.
    • Explanation: Building out-of-town shopping malls, retail parks, and supermarkets on the fringe pulls consumer footfall and tax revenue away from traditional city centers, leading to downtown shop closures. Additionally, councils must fund expensive extensions of water mains, sewage pipes, and power grids across sprawling distances.
  • Environmental Impact:
    • Impact: Loss of wildlife habitats and increased flood risk.
    • Explanation: Developing greenfield sites destroys natural ecosystems, woodlands, and hedgerows. Covering soil with impermeable tarmac roads, driveways, and concrete foundations prevents rainwater infiltration, dramatically increasing surface runoff and flood risk in low-lying areas.
