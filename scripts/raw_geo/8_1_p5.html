Activity 1

  1. What is development?
  2. Give two examples of development in a low-income country.
  3. The UNDP stresses the importance of a balance between which three types of development?
  4. What is the reason for calculating GNI data at purchasing power parity?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  1. Development is a process of change for the better, representing an improvement in the quality of life and standard of living. It involves not only increasing wealth but also improvements in social, economic, environmental, and political aspects of life.
  2. Examples could include: local food supply improving due to investment in farm machinery/fertilisers; the electricity grid extending to rural areas; improved literacy levels; or a new road/railway improving accessibility to a remote province.
  3. Social, economic, and environmental sustainability.
  4. GNI at purchasing power parity (PPP) is calculated to take into account the differences in the cost of living between countries, providing a more accurate reflection of living standards and purchasing power than standard GNI data.

Activity 2

Table 8.2 Key development indicators by world region
  1. Define adult literacy.
  2. Which world regions have the highest and lowest infant mortality rates?
  3. List the four main influences on life expectancy.
  4. Using the data from Table 8.2 (page 180), draw a scatter graph to show the relationship between life expectancy at birth and infant mortality rate. Insert a line of best fit and comment on the relationship between the two variables.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  1. Adult literacy is the ability to read and write among adults.
  2. The highest infant mortality rates are found in Africa, particularly Sub-Saharan Africa. The lowest infant mortality rates are typically found in Europe and North America.
  3. The four main influences on life expectancy are: the incidence of disease (e.g., malaria); physical environmental conditions (e.g., low rainfall); human environmental conditions (e.g., pollution); and personal lifestyle (e.g., smoking).
  4. Graph description: The scatter graph would show a strong negative correlation between life expectancy at birth and infant mortality rate. As life expectancy increases, the infant mortality rate tends to decrease. This inverse relationship indicates that countries with better healthcare, nutrition, and living standards typically enjoy both higher life expectancies and lower infant mortality rates.

Activity 3

Figure 8.7 Variations in the Human Development Index (HDI)
  1. Which indicators of development are combined to form the Human Development Index?
  2. Look at Figure 8.7 and briefly describe the distribution of countries according to the Human Development Index.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  1. The Human Development Index (HDI) combines a long and healthy life (life expectancy), knowledge (expected and mean years of schooling/literacy), and a decent standard of living (GNI per capita).
  2. Generally, the distribution shows very high HDI in North America, Europe, Australia/New Zealand, and parts of East Asia. Low HDI countries are heavily concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa. Medium and High HDI countries are widely distributed across South America, Asia, and North Africa.
