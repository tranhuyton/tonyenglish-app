Managing the Impacts of Tectonic Hazards

Master the 4 Ps management framework, the Park Model disaster response curve, seismic engineering, and volcano mitigation case studies.

1. The 3 Ps / 4 Ps Management Framework

While humans cannot prevent tectonic plates from moving, rigorous hazard management minimizes societal vulnerability and loss of life. Cambridge IGCSE Geography structures management around the 4 Ps Framework:

1. Prediction

Using science to identify when and where an event will occur. Highly effective for volcanoes (gas, tremors, bulging), but virtually impossible for earthquakes beyond historical probability.

2. Protection

Designing physical defenses and engineering structures that withstand shockwaves or divert volcanic material (earthquake-resistant high-rises, lava diversion ditches, seawalls).

3. Preparation

Training communities and authorities before a crisis strikes. Includes annual evacuation drills, early warning alerts (ShakeAlert), emergency Grab-and-Go backpacks, and medical stockpiling.

4. Planning

Strategic spatial land-use zoning preventing construction of nuclear facilities, schools, or hospitals on fault lines, liquefaction plains, or lahar drainage channels.

2. The Park Model (Disaster Response Curve)

The Park Model (Figure 4.18) illustrates the trajectory of quality of life following a hazard event, displaying the progression from emergency relief through recovery to permanent reconstruction:

Interactive Conceptual Model

The Park Model: Disaster Response Curve

Quality of Life Trajectory Over Time

Phases of the Park Model

  1. Pre-Disaster Normality: Baseline quality of life prior to the sudden tectonic shock.
  2. Immediate Impact & Disruption: Hours to days following the event. Quality of life plunges as infrastructure collapses, power and water systems fail, and human casualties mount. The steepness of the drop depends on hazard magnitude and building vulnerability.
  3. Relief Phase (Hours to Days): Immediate search-and-rescue teams (sniffing dogs, thermal cameras), medical triage field hospitals, distribution of clean bottled water, blankets, and emergency tents to prevent dehydration and hypothermia.
  4. Rehabilitation Phase (Weeks to Months): Re-establishing essential lifelines: reconnecting electrical grids, repairing ruptured water mains, clearing blocked transportation arteries, reopening schools in temporary facilities, and providing psychological trauma counseling.
  5. Reconstruction Phase (Months to Years): Permanent replacement of destroyed housing and infrastructure. Three potential outcomes emerge:
    • Build Back Better (HICs): Upgraded building codes, superior transport, and resilient economic regeneration (surpassing pre-disaster quality of life).
    • Return to Normal: Restoring conditions to the pre-disaster status quo.
    • Persistent Deficit (LICs): In severely impoverished nations like Haiti, depleted capital, institutional instability, and secondary disasters leave quality of life lower than before the earthquake struck.

3. Earthquake Mitigation & Structural Engineering

Earthquakes cannot be predicted accurately in time or magnitude. Therefore, mitigation relies on engineering buildings to flex rather than fracture during seismic shaking:

🏢 Engineering Features of an Earthquake-Resistant Skyscraper

🏙️ High-Tech Engineering (HICs: Japan, USA)

  • Tuned Mass Dampers: Enormous suspended counterweight pendulums (e.g. 660-tonne sphere in Taipei 101) that sway in the opposite direction of seismic shock to stabilize skyscrapers.
  • Base Isolation Bearings: Laminated lead-rubber pads installed between bedrock and foundations that decouple the building from ground motion, absorbing up to 80% of horizontal shear.
  • Steel Cross-Bracing: Diagonal lattice structures that reinforce exterior frames against shearing distortion.
  • Pipelines on Rollers: Oil and water pipes mounted on Teflon slider brackets (Figure 4.20) allow ground slip without buckling.

🏡 Appropriate Low-Cost Technology (LICs)

  • Lightweight Corrugated Iron Roofs: Replacing heavy clay or concrete tiles with corrugated metal sheets prevents crushing deaths during roof collapse.
  • Bamboo & Wire-Mesh Reinforcement: Weaving flexible bamboo lattice into mud or adobe brick walls holds crumbling debris together.
  • Discarded Tires as Base Isolators: Packing sand inside used vehicle tires placed beneath foundations creates cheap, effective shock absorbers.
  • Straw-Bale Construction: Lightweight, compressed straw blocks tied with wire provide exceptional flexibility and thermal insulation.

4. Volcano Management & Case Studies

Unlike earthquakes, volcanic eruptions are preceded by measurable physical and chemical warning signals, making scientific prediction highly reliable:

Volcano Monitoring Instruments & Techniques

  • Tiltmeters & GPS Networks: Detect micro-changes in ground slope and surface swelling as magma inflates the volcanic cone prior to eruption.
  • Gas Spectrometry (COSPEC): Measures rising emissions of sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and carbon dioxide (CO₂), signaling that magma is nearing the surface.
  • Seismometer Swarms: Tracks harmonic tremors—continuous rhythmic vibrations caused by magma fracturing rock as it ascends the conduit.
  • Thermal Satellite Sensors: Infrared satellite imagery (e.g. Sentinel, MODIS) spots subterranean hot spots before visual eruption commences.

🌋 Case Study: Mount Etna, Sicily (HIC Volcano Management)

  • Tectonic Setting: Europe's most active stratovolcano, situated at the convergent margin between the African and Eurasian plates (Figure 4.23).
  • Lava Diversion Engineering: In 1992, engineers successfully protected the town of Zafferana by dropping concrete blocks and using precision explosives to breach lava tubes, diverting molten flows into artificial channels.
  • Monitoring & Hazard Zoning: Monitored 24/7 by the National Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology (INGV). Constant GPS, seismic swarms, and gas sensors ensure zero fatalities in modern tourist seasons.
  • Impact: Eruptions regularly damage ski cable cars (Sapienza Refuge, Figure 4.25) and agricultural vineyards, but comprehensive compensation and timely evacuations protect lives.

🇭🇹 Case Study: Tiburon Peninsula Earthquake, Haiti (2021)

  • Parameters: 14 August 2021 | Mw 7.2 | Depth 10 km on the Enriquillo-Plantain Garden fault (Figure 4.21).
  • Compound Disasters: Tropical Storm Grace struck just 48 hours later, triggering thousands of landslides and washing away makeshift rescue tents. Over 137,000 houses damaged and 2,248 killed.
  • Management Failure: Political paralysis (the President had been assassinated weeks earlier), extreme poverty, and gang control of national transport routes crippled search-and-rescue (Figure 4.22), leaving Haiti trapped in the Park Model's permanent deficit.
