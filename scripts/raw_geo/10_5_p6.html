TASK 1: Study Source A

Study Source A: The variation in daily and yearly solar radiation patterns around the world

Source A: The variation in daily and yearly solar radiation patterns around the world
  1. a. In pairs, discuss which regions are most likely to support the use of solar power as a reliable energy source. Briefly give your reasoning, using information from the source to support your answer.
  2. b. Would the UK be able to depend on solar power to meet any energy deficits? Consider the information in the source and how climate and seasonal changes would influence their ability to rely on solar power.
💡 View Suggested Answers

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Regions most likely to support solar power as a reliable energy source:

  • Data from Source A: Regions receiving the highest solar irradiation levels—daily totals of 6.2 to 7.4+ kWh/m² and yearly totals from 2,264 to 2,702+ kWh/m² (colored in deep red, magenta, and purple on the map).
  • Key geographical regions:
    • North Africa and the Sahara Desert (e.g. Algeria, Egypt, Libya, Sudan).
    • The Middle East / Arabian Peninsula (e.g. Saudi Arabia, UAE, Oman).
    • Southern Africa (e.g. Namibia, Botswana, South Africa).
    • Southwestern North America & Northern Mexico (e.g. Mojave and Sonoran Deserts).
    • Western South America (the Atacama Desert in Chile/Peru).
    • Central and Western Australia.
  • Reasoning: These regions are located along sub-tropical high-pressure belts where sinking dry air produces perpetual clear, cloud-free skies and very low humidity throughout the year, ensuring high, consistent solar radiation year-round.

b. Would the UK be able to depend on solar power to meet energy deficits?

  • Data from Source A: The UK is situated in the low-radiation band (colored in teal/light green), receiving only 2.6 to 3.0 kWh/m² per day and 949 to 1,095 kWh/m² per year—less than half the radiation received by sub-tropical desert regions.
  • Climate influence: The UK experiences a temperate maritime climate with frequent cloud cover, mist, and Atlantic frontal rainfall, substantially diffusing and reducing direct solar insolation.
  • Seasonal mismatch: During UK winters, when energy demand for indoor heating and lighting surges to its peak, day lengths are extremely short (under 8 hours of daylight) and the solar altitude angle is very low, producing minimal solar power. Peak solar generation occurs during long summer days when national heating demand is lowest.
  • Conclusion: The UK cannot depend on solar power alone to resolve energy deficits. Solar can contribute to a diversified grid during summer, but the UK must rely on winter-peaking wind energy, nuclear, and energy storage to maintain supply security.
TASK 2: Study Source B

Study Source B: Cartoon depicting a problem arising out of energy insecurity

Source B: Cartoon depicting a problem arising out of energy insecurity

In pairs, discuss what this cartoon is depicting. Then, in 100 to 200 words, write down the message of the cartoon and explain the possible reasons for such a situation developing.

💡 View Suggested Answers

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

What the cartoon depicts:

A parent and child bundled in heavy winter coats and hats, shivering in a sleeping bag in snowy freezing weather. They are huddled against a domestic radiator equipped with a coin-operated prepayment meter. The adult holds a cardboard sign pleading "PLEASE HELP", while an empty begging bowl sits before them on the snow.

Model Response (approx. 160 words): Message and Reasons for Energy Insecurity:

Message of the Cartoon:
The cartoon illustrates the acute reality of fuel poverty and energy insecurity within modern societies. While heating infrastructure exists (the radiator), access to warmth has been commercialised and paywalled behind a prepayment coin meter. Because the impoverished family has no money in their begging bowl, they are subjected to freezing conditions in their own home.

Reasons for this Situation:
1. Spike in Wholesale Energy Tariffs: Geopolitical shocks, international gas supply disruptions, and inflation drive domestic heating tariffs beyond affordable levels.
2. Low Incomes & Cost-of-Living Squeeze: Poor families cannot keep pace with surging electricity and gas prices, forcing severe trade-offs between heating and eating.
3. Prepayment Meter Penalties: Low-income households are frequently assigned coin/key prepayment meters that charge higher unit rates and automatically cut off heat when credit runs out (self-disconnection).
4. Inadequate Home Insulation: Substandard housing leaks warmth rapidly, magnifying the cost required to achieve livable room temperatures.
TASK 3: Study Sources C, D, and E

Study Sources C, D, and E: National energy mixes for the UK (HIC), Malaysia (MIC), and Zimbabwe (LIC) in 2022

Source C: The energy mix for the UK in 2022 (HIC)
Source D: The energy mix for Malaysia in 2022 (MIC)
Source E: The energy mix for Zimbabwe in 2022 (LIC)
  1. a. Which country has the highest reliance on coal?
  2. b. Which country has the most diverse energy mix?
  3. c. Compare the energy mixes between the UK, Malaysia, and Zimbabwe.
  4. d. Suggest why these energy mixes are so different between the three countries.
  5. e. In pairs or small groups, carry out research into the mix of energy of your own country, giving reasons for the mix that is used.
💡 View Suggested Answers

Teacher's Suggested Answer & Guidelines

a. Highest reliance on coal:

  • Zimbabwe (50%) has the highest reliance on coal, where half of all electricity/energy is coal-fired.
  • Comparison: Malaysia relies on coal for 36%, whereas the UK has almost eliminated coal (coal & oil together account for only 2%).

b. Most diverse energy mix:

  • The UK (HIC) has by far the most diverse energy mix, comprising 7 distinct energy sources: Gas (38%), Wind (25%), Nuclear (15%), Bioenergy (11%), Solar/HEP/marine (6%), Other (3%), and Coal/oil (2%). No single source dominates more than 38% of total supply.
  • In contrast, Zimbabwe relies almost entirely on just two sources (Coal 50% + HEP 45% = 95%).

c. Comparing the energy mixes between the UK, Malaysia, and Zimbabwe:

  • Fossil Fuel Dependence: Malaysia has the highest overall fossil fuel reliance (75%: Gas 39% + Coal 36%). Zimbabwe has 50% fossil reliance (all Coal). The UK has the lowest fossil share at 40% (Gas 38% + Coal/oil 2%).
  • Renewable Energy: The UK leads in modern diversified renewables (42% total: Wind 25%, Bioenergy 11%, Solar/HEP/marine 6%). Zimbabwe's renewable power is almost entirely Hydroelectric (45% HEP, 4% Bioenergy, 1% Solar). Malaysia has 22% renewable share (16% HEP, 6% Solar).
  • Nuclear Power: Only the UK utilizes nuclear energy (15%); neither Malaysia nor Zimbabwe utilizes nuclear power (0%).
  • Natural Gas: Both the UK (38%) and Malaysia (39%) rely heavily on natural gas as their largest single electricity source, whereas Zimbabwe uses 0% gas.

d. Reasons for the differences in energy mixes:

  • Level of Economic Development & Capital Investment (HIC vs. LIC): Constructing offshore wind farms, nuclear power stations, and biomass facilities requires enormous capital, advanced technology, and skilled engineers that the UK can finance. Zimbabwe (LIC) faces capital constraints and debt, restricting investments to maintaining existing coal mines and legacy dams.
  • Domestic Natural Resource Endowments:
    • UK: Extensive shallow coastal seas provide ideal conditions for offshore wind; historic reserves of North Sea gas.
    • Malaysia: Large offshore natural gas reserves in the South China Sea, coupled with high tropical rainfall and river valleys supporting HEP (e.g. Bakun Dam).
    • Zimbabwe: Vast domestic coal deposits at Hwange basin, and access to the Zambezi River hosting the massive Kariba Dam hydroelectric project.
  • Government Policy & Climate Commitments: The UK enforces strict carbon emissions legislation, legally binding Net Zero targets, and heavy carbon taxes, prompting the phaseout of coal. Developing countries prioritise low-cost, reliable baseload power to fuel rapid industrialisation and expand basic grid access to rural citizens.

e. Guidelines for researching your own country's energy mix (e.g. Vietnam / Regional comparisons):

  • Vietnam Energy Mix: Coal (~38-45%), Hydroelectric (~28-35%), Solar & Wind (~12-14%), Gas (~8-10%).
  • Key drivers: Extensive mountainous river basins enable major HEP generation (Son La, Hoa Binh); high solar irradiance and coastal winds in Ninh Thuan/Binh Thuan attracted rapid renewable investment; coal serves as affordable baseload power for booming industrial manufacturing.
