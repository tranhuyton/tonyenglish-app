Activity 1

  • 1. Suggest reasons why international agreements to manage the impact of climate change have been unsuccessful.
  • 2. Explain how green infrastructure and water- and energy-conservation can be examples of adaptation and mitigation.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Agreements are often unsuccessful because they lack strict, binding enforcement mechanisms and differentiated responsibilities can lead to disputes. Some countries prioritize short-term economic growth over environmental commitments. Furthermore, key polluting nations have sometimes withdrawn from agreements (e.g., the USA temporarily leaving the Paris Agreement), and global greenhouse gas emissions have continued to rise overall.
  • 2. As adaptation, green infrastructure (like urban trees) helps cool cities during heatwaves, while water conservation helps communities cope with increased droughts and shifting rainfall patterns. As mitigation, green infrastructure acts as a carbon sink by absorbing CO2 from the atmosphere, and energy conservation directly reduces the burning of fossil fuels, thereby lowering greenhouse gas emissions.

Activity 2

Figure 5.13 Areas at risk of flooding in Bangladesh under different sea level rise scenarios
  • 1. Describe the distribution and type of flood risk in Bangladesh, as shown in Figure 5.13 (page 120).
  • 2. Distinguish between adaptation and mitigation strategies that have been used in Bangladesh.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Over 50 per cent of the population in Bangladesh faces a 'high' exposure to flooding. Two-thirds of the country is low-lying (less than 5m above sea level), making extensive coastal areas highly vulnerable to coastal flooding, storm surges from cyclones, and sea-level rise. Inland areas are also subject to severe river floods.
  • 2. Adaptation strategies in Bangladesh aim to cope with climate impacts; these include building higher flood embankments, constructing cyclone and flood shelters, and developing stress-tolerant, salt-tolerant, and drought-resistant crop varieties. Mitigation strategies aim to reduce the causes of climate change, such as planting millions of trees (including mangroves) to absorb carbon and transitioning to renewable energy and greater energy efficiency to cut greenhouse gas emissions.
