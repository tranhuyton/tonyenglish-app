Activity 1

  • 1. Comment on the value of tropical rainforests for human populations.
  • 2. Outline the main impacts of deforestation on the natural environment.
  • 3. Explain the main causes of deforestation in Brazil.
  • 4. Comment on the trends in deforestation in Brazil.
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Tropical rainforests provide indigenous communities with food, shelter, and traditional medicine. Globally, they are invaluable for regulating the Earth's climate, acting as a massive carbon sink, preventing floods, and providing commercial resources like timber, nuts, and genetic material for modern pharmaceuticals.
  • 2. The main impacts include a severe loss of biodiversity and species extinction, disruption of the local and global water cycles, heavy soil erosion due to the removal of protective vegetation, and significant contributions to global warming through the release of stored carbon.
  • 3. Deforestation in Brazil is primarily driven by land clearance for cattle ranching, large-scale commercial agriculture (especially soy production), legal and illegal logging, mining for minerals, and large infrastructure projects like roads and hydroelectric dams.
  • 4. Historically, deforestation rates in Brazil peaked in the early 2000s, followed by a significant decline due to stricter enforcement and conservation policies. However, in recent years, deforestation trends have fluctuated and sometimes increased again due to changing political priorities and economic pressure to expand agriculture.

Activity 2

  • 1. What was the main threat to the Danum Valley before the late 1980s?
  • 2. Why is the DVCA important for the conservation of species?
💡 View Suggested Answers
  • 1. Before the late 1980s, the primary threat to the Danum Valley was large-scale commercial logging, which targeted the area for its highly valuable timber resources.
  • 2. The Danum Valley Conservation Area (DVCA) is critically important because it protects one of the most extensive remaining tracts of primary lowland dipterocarp forest in Borneo. It acts as an undisturbed sanctuary for many highly endangered and endemic species, including the Bornean orangutan, pygmy elephant, and clouded leopard.
