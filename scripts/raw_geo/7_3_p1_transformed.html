1. Urban Housing & The Brownfield vs. Greenfield Dilemma

Nghe phần này

As urban populations expand, municipal governments face an acute land-use dilemma: should cities expand outward onto undeveloped farmland (greenfield sites) or redevelop abandoned industrial land in the inner core (brownfield sites)?

Figure 7.16: Industrial heritage conversion of Lucy's Iron Foundry into modern residential apartments. A classic example of sustainable brownfield regeneration in Oxford, UK.
Site Type Advantages (Pros) Disadvantages (Cons)
BROWNFIELD SITES
(Abandoned factories, rail yards, docks) 		• Revitalizes derelict inner-city eyesores.
• Pre-existing infrastructure (roads, piped water, electricity grid) reduces connection costs.
• Conserves greenbelt farmland and stops urban sprawl.
• Near CBD jobs; reduces commuting distances. 		• Toxic soil decontamination (lead, chemicals) is exceptionally expensive.
• Demolition of old Victorian structures adds financial costs.
• Cramped plot shapes restrict building design and parking.
GREENFIELD SITES
(Farmland & countryside on urban fringe) 		• Land purchase price is significantly cheaper per hectare.
• Blank canvas allows flexible layout, spacious gardens, and ample car parking.
• Clean pleasant environment away from inner-city congestion and noise. 		• Irreversible loss of productive agricultural land and wildlife habitats.
• Encourages automobile dependency and heavy highway congestion.
• High municipal cost to extend water mains, sewers, and electricity out to the fringe.

3. Sustainable Urban Transport Strategies

Nghe phần này

Traffic congestion in cities costs hundreds of billions in lost economic productivity and causes severe respiratory illness. Cities deploy combined "carrot and stick" strategies:

"Carrot" Strategies (Incentivizing Transit & Cycling)
  • Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) - Curitiba, Brazil: Tube stations with elevated boarding platforms allow level entry in seconds. Bi-articulated buses carry 300 passengers on dedicated median busways, carrying 2 million passengers daily.
  • Integrated Rail & Transit Hubs: Seamless oyster/smartcard ticketing linking metro, trams, and ferries.
  • Active Travel Infrastructure - Copenhagen: Over 400 km of segregated bicycle superhighways with synchronized traffic lights ("Green Waves" timed at 20 km/h for cyclists).
"Stick" Strategies (Restricting Private Cars)
  • Congestion Charging - London ULEZ: Automatic number-plate recognition cameras charge £15/day to enter the central zone, reducing traffic volume by 20% and cutting CO₂.
  • Vehicle Quota System - Singapore: Bidders must purchase an expensive 10-year Certificate of Entitlement (COE) often exceeding $100,000, legally capping car growth at zero percent.
  • Pedestrianized City Centers - Freiburg, Germany: Cars banned in the historic medieval core; ring roads funnel vehicles to peripheral park-and-ride garages.

5. Environmental Hazards in Megacities: Air Quality in Delhi

Nghe phần này

Rapid urbanization without stringent environmental regulation leads to severe pollution crises. Delhi, India's capital region of 33 million residents, faces the most dangerous seasonal air pollution in the world.

Figure 7.24: Seasonal variations in air quality in Delhi. Air Quality Index (AQI) spikes into the "Severe / Hazardous" zone (450–500+) during October–December due to post-harvest stubble burning and winter thermal inversions.
Delhi's Multi-Tiered Management Interventions:
  • CNG Fuel Mandate: Entire fleet of 100,000 public buses and auto-rickshaws converted from diesel to clean Compressed Natural Gas (CNG).
  • The Odd-Even Rationing Scheme: Private vehicles with odd or even registration plates permitted on roads on alternating days during peak winter smog.
  • Rapid Expansion of Delhi Metro: Now over 390 km across 12 lines, eliminating millions of motorized road trips daily.
  • Smog Towers & Stubble Subsidies: Massive air-purifying filtration towers erected at major intersections; subsidies provided to farmers for bio-decomposer sprays to stop crop burning.
💡 Cambridge IGCSE Examination Strategy: Topic 7.3

When answering questions on urban management:
1. Cite named sustainable schemes: Explicitly mention Curitiba's BRT system, Singapore's green roof requirements, or Shanghai's 25-million population cap and Yangshupu Power Plant conversion.
2. Contrast Brownfield vs. Greenfield: Always demonstrate balanced evaluation—praising brownfield for saving greenbelt farmland while acknowledging site contamination cleanup costs.
3. Use the 3 Pillars framework: Structure long 7-mark answers around Environmental, Economic, and Social sustainability to guarantee top-band marks.
