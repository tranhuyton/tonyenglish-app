📏 1. Physical Quantities & Measurement Techniques (P1.1)

Measuring Instruments

  • Rulers: Precision of 1 mm (0.1 cm). To avoid parallax error, always position your eye directly perpendicular (90°) to the scale.
  • Measuring Cylinders: Measures liquid volume. Read horizontally from the bottom of the meniscus at eye level.
  • Digital Timers & Clocks: Measures time intervals in seconds. Human reaction time is approximately 0.2 s.

Technique: Measuring Multiples

When measuring tiny quantities, measure a large number of multiples and divide:

Sheet Thickness: Measure thickness of 500 sheets of paper with a ruler, then divide total thickness by 500.
Pendulum Period (T): Time 20 complete oscillations (t₂₀), then calculate T = t₂₀ / 20 to reduce reaction time errors.

Supplement: Scalars vs Vectors

A scalar quantity has magnitude (size) only. A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.

Type Definition Required Syllabus Examples
Scalars Magnitude only Distance, Speed, Time, Mass, Energy, Temperature
Vectors Magnitude AND direction Force, Weight, Velocity, Acceleration, Gravitational field strength

🚨 CAMBRIDGE EXAM PITFALL: Check the Y-Axis!

A common loss of marks occurs when candidates fail to check the vertical axis label. A horizontal flat line on a Distance-Time graph means the object is Stationary (v = 0). However, on a Speed-Time graph, a horizontal flat line means the object is moving at Constant Speed (a = 0)!

⚖️ 3. Mass, Weight & Density (P1.3 & P1.4)

DENSITY (ρ = m / V)

ρ = m / V
  • ρ (Density): Mass per unit volume (kg/m³ or g/cm³).
  • m: Mass (kg or g). • V: Volume (m³ or cm³).
  • Water has density ρ = 1.0 g/cm³. Objects with ρ < 1.0 float in water.

WEIGHT (W = m × g)

W = m × g
  • W (Weight): Gravitational force in Newtons (N).
  • m (Mass): Quantity of matter in kg.
  • g: Gravitational field strength (g ≈ 9.8 N/kg on Earth; ~1.6 N/kg on Moon).

⚔️ Distinguishing Mass and Weight (Crucial Paper 4 Questions)

Criteria Mass (Khối lượng) Weight (Trọng lượng)
Definition The quantity of matter in an object The gravitational force acting on a mass
SI Unit Kilogram (kg) Newton (N)
Quantity Type Scalar (magnitude only) Vector (acts towards centre of gravity)
Variation Constant everywhere in the universe Changes depending on g (e.g. Moon vs Earth)

⚙️ 4. Forces, Hooke's Law, Moments & Pressure (P1.5 & P1.7)

Hooke's Law (F = k × x)

The extension of a spring is directly proportional to the applied force, provided the limit of proportionality is not exceeded: F = k × x (k = spring constant in N/m or N/cm).

  • Linear Region: The spring obeys Hooke's law (elastic deformation).
  • Limit of Proportionality: The point on the graph beyond which extension is no longer proportional to load (red dot).
  • Curved Region: The spring is permanently deformed and will not return to its original length (plastic deformation).

⚖️ Turning Forces (Moments)

Moment = Force × Distance (M = F × d)

d must be the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. Unit: N•m.
Principle of Moments: In equilibrium, Total Clockwise Moments = Total Anticlockwise Moments.

🔨 Pressure (Áp suất)

Pressure = Force / Area (p = F / A)

Units: Pascals (Pa) or N/m².
• Large Area = Low Pressure: Snowshoes, broad tractor caterpillar tracks avoid sinking.
• Small Area = High Pressure: Sharp kitchen knives, drawing pins penetrate surfaces easily.

🔋 5. Energy Stores & Transfer Pathways (P1.6)

Principle of Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred from one store to another. The total energy in any closed system remains strictly constant.

📦 Energy Stores

  • Kinetic: In moving objects (½mv²).
  • Gravitational Potential: Objects raised above ground (mgh).
  • Chemical: In food, fuels, batteries.
  • Elastic (Strain): In stretched or compressed springs.
  • Nuclear: Stored in atomic nuclei.
  • Internal (Thermal): Kinetic and potential energy of particles in matter.
  • Electrostatic: Attracting or repelling charges.

🛤️ Energy Transfer Pathways

  • Mechanically: By a force acting through a distance (W = F × d).
  • Electrically: By electric current moving charge.
  • By Heating: Due to temperature differences (conduction, convection).
  • By Radiation: Transferred by electromagnetic waves (light, IR) or sound.

🎢 6. Kinetic & Gravitational Potential Energy

🎢 Interactive Pendulum (Energy Conservation)

Hover over positions A, B, and C on the pendulum to examine the continuous interconversion of GPE and KE.

Hover over positions A, B, or C 👆

Discover how energy exchanges between Gravitational Potential Energy and Kinetic Energy while total mechanical energy remains constant.

Position A (Maximum Height)

• GPE (Gravitational Potential Energy): MAXIMUM (ΔEp = mgh)
• KE (Kinetic Energy): ZERO (v = 0 at instantaneous stop)
• All kinetic energy has converted into gravitational potential energy.

Position B (Lowest Point)

• GPE: MINIMUM (h = 0 reference level)
• KE: MAXIMUM (Speed v is greatest!)
• By energy conservation: Maximum KE = Lost GPE (½mv² = mgh).

Position C (Maximum Height)

• GPE: MAXIMUM (Same vertical height as A in ideal conditions)
• KE: ZERO (Bob momentarily stops before reversing)
• In reality, air resistance causes mechanical energy to transfer slowly into thermal energy (damping).

Gravitational Potential Energy (ΔEp = mgh)

ΔEp = m × g × Δh
  • m: Mass in kg • g: 9.8 N/kg • Δh: Vertical height raised in metres.
  • Energy stored by an object when elevated in a gravitational field.

Kinetic Energy (Ek = ½mv²)

Ek = ½ × m × v²
  • m: Mass in kg • v: Speed in m/s.
  • Crucial Factor: Notice v is squared (v²)! Doubling speed quadruples (× 4) kinetic energy!

Work Done (W = F × d = ΔE)

• Formula: W = F × d (Work = Force × distance in direction of force)
• Units: Joules (J) = Newtons (N) × metres (m)
• Energy Transfer: Doing work transfers energy: W = ΔE.

Power (P = W / t = ΔE / t)

• Definition: The rate of doing work or rate of energy transfer per unit time.
• Units: Watts (W) = Joules per second (J/s)
• 1 Watt = 1 Joule transferred in 1 second.

Efficiency Calculation

• Efficiency (%) = (Useful energy output / Total energy input) × 100%
• Power Ratio: Efficiency (%) = (Useful power output / Total power input) × 100%
• Efficiency can NEVER exceed 100% (due to energy conservation).

🚨 CAMBRIDGE EXAM PITFALL: Walking vs Running Up Stairs

A classic Cambridge Paper 4 question: A student of mass 50 kg walks up a flight of stairs in 10 seconds, then runs up the same stairs in 4 seconds.
• Work Done: Exactly the SAME for both! (W = ΔEp = mgh, weight and vertical height are unchanged).
• Power: Much GREATER when running! (P = W / t, since running takes less time t, power output is larger!).

Fossil Fuels Non-renewable The Sun Reliable on demand Emits CO₂ (global warming) & SO₂
Nuclear Fission Non-renewable Uranium nuclei Huge energy density, no CO₂ Radioactive waste disposal
Hydroelectric Renewable The Sun (Water cycle) Rapid start-up time Flooding valleys & displacement

Solar Renewable The Sun (Radiation) Abundant, remote power Zero power at night

Core Syllabus Fact: The Sun is the source of all our energy resources EXCEPT Geothermal, Nuclear, and Tidal.
