Space physics

P6.1 The Solar System

🟦 CORE

  • 1. Describe the Solar System as containing:
    (a) one star, the Sun
    (b) the eight named planets and know their order from the Sun
    (c) minor planets that orbit the Sun, including dwarf planets such as Pluto and asteroids in the asteroid belt
    (d) moons, that orbit the planets.[cite: 1518]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • 2. Define orbital speed from the equation v = 2πr / T where r is the radius of the orbit and T is the orbital period.[cite: 1518]
  • 3. Know that the strength of the Sun's gravitational field decreases and that the orbital speeds of the planets decrease as the distance from the Sun increases.[cite: 1518]

P6.2 Stars and the Universe

🟦 CORE

  • P6.2.1 The Sun as a star:
  • 1. Know that: (a) the Sun is the closest star to the Earth (b) astronomical distances can be measured in light-years.[cite: 1518]
  • P6.2.3 Galaxies and the Universe:
  • 1. Know that: (a) galaxies are each made up of many billions of stars (b) the Sun is a star in the Milky Way (c) other stars are much further away.[cite: 1518]
  • 2. Know that the Milky Way is one of many billions of galaxies and that its diameter is approximately 100,000 light-years.[cite: 1518]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • P6.2.1 The Sun as a star:
  • 2. Calculate the time it takes light to travel a significant distance.[cite: 1518]
  • 3. Know that the Sun contains most of the mass of the Solar System and this explains why the planets orbit the Sun.[cite: 1518]
  • 4. Know that the force that keeps an object in orbit around the Sun is due to gravitational attraction.[cite: 1518]
  • 5. Know that the Sun is a star of medium size, mostly hydrogen and helium, radiating infrared, visible and ultraviolet.[cite: 1518]
  • 6. Know that stars are powered by nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium.[cite: 1518]
  • P6.2.2 Life cycle of stars:
  • 1. Know that stable stars are formed as protostars from interstellar clouds of gas and dust.[cite: 1518]
  • 2. Know that next stages depend on mass: (a) small mass: red giant → white dwarf + planetary nebula (b) large mass: red supergiant → supernova → neutron star (c) very large mass: red supergiant → supernova → black hole.[cite: 1518]
  • 3. Know that the nebula from a supernova may form new stars with orbiting planets.[cite: 1518]
  • P6.2.3 Galaxies and the Universe:
  • 3. Know that the Big Bang Theory states: (a) the Universe expanded from a single point of high density/temperature (b) is still expanding (c) is ~13.8 billion years old.[cite: 1518]
