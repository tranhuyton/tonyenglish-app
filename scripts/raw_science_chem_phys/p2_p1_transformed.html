🧊 1. Kinetic Particle Model of Matter (P2.1)

States of Matter: Arrangement, Forces & Motion

State Arrangement Separation & Forces Particle Motion Shape & Volume
Solid Regular lattice structure Tightly packed, very strong forces Vibrate about fixed positions Fixed shape & fixed volume
Liquid Random / Irregular arrangement Close together, moderate forces Slide past one another Takes container shape, fixed volume
Gas Completely random & far apart Negligible forces except on collision Move rapidly in all directions Expands to fill any container

💨 Brownian Motion

The random, zigzag motion of visible smoke particles or pollen grains viewed under a microscope.
• Cause: Result of continuous, random collisions with microscopic, fast-moving, invisible air/water molecules.
• Evidence: Direct evidence for the kinetic particle theory of matter.

🎈 Gas Pressure & Temperature

• Origin of Pressure: Gas particles constantly collide with the interior walls of their container, exerting an outward force per unit area (p = F / A).
• Effect of Heating: Increasing temperature increases the average kinetic energy and speed of particles. They collide more frequently and with greater force, increasing pressure.

🌡️ 2. Thermal Expansion & Evaporation (P2.2)

Thermal Expansion in Solids, Liquids & Gases

When matter is heated, particles gain kinetic energy, vibrate or move faster, pushing slightly further apart. Expansion Order: Gases > Liquids > Solids.

Solids: Expand least due to strong inter-particle bonds. (Railway expansion joints prevent buckling).
Liquids: Expand more than solids. Used in liquid-in-glass thermometers.
Gases: Expand most because intermolecular forces are virtually zero.

💨 Why Does Evaporation Cause Cooling? (Crucial Paper 4 Question)

Candidates must memorise this precise 3-step explanation for full marks:

  1. Particles in a liquid have a range of different speeds and kinetic energies.
  2. The most energetic particles near the surface overcome attractive forces from neighbours and escape into the vapour phase.
  3. The particles left behind have a lower average kinetic energy. Since temperature is directly proportional to average kinetic energy, the liquid's temperature decreases.

Factors Increasing Evaporation Rate:

🌡️
Higher Temperature

More particles have sufficient KE to escape.

📏
Larger Surface Area

More surface particles available to escape.

💨
Draught / Wind

Blows escaped vapour away, preventing condensation.

Thermal energy transfers by Conduction (solids), Convection (fluids), and Radiation (vacuum & infrared waves).

🧱 Conduction (Solids)

Occurs mainly in solids. Particles at the heated end vibrate more vigorously and collide with neighbouring particles, transferring kinetic energy along the material.

🚨 CAMBRIDGE PAPER 4 MARKING SCHEME FOR METALS:
Metals are exceptionally good thermal conductors because they contain free (delocalised) electrons. These electrons absorb thermal energy, gain kinetic energy, and move rapidly throughout the metallic lattice, colliding with distant ions and transferring energy much faster.

💧 Convection (Fluids)

Occurs ONLY in fluids (liquids and gases). Heat is transferred by the physical movement of the fluid itself.

  • When heated, the fluid expands and becomes less dense.
  • The less dense, warmer fluid rises.
  • Denser, cooler fluid sinks to replace it, setting up a continuous convection current.

☀️ Radiation (Infrared Waves)

Transfer of thermal energy by electromagnetic waves (infrared radiation). It does NOT require particles and can travel through a vacuum.

  • All solar energy reaches Earth across space purely by radiation.
  • All objects emit thermal radiation; hotter objects emit more radiation per second.

🎨 Surfaces & Thermal Radiation

⚫ Matte Black Surfaces

  • Absorption: Best absorber of thermal radiation (heats up quickest).
  • Emission: Best emitter of thermal radiation (cools down quickest).
  • Reflection: Worst reflector.

⚪ Shiny White / Silver Surfaces

  • Absorption: Poor absorber of thermal radiation.
  • Emission: Poor emitter of thermal radiation (retains heat inside).
  • Reflection: Best reflector of thermal radiation.

🧊 Real-world Application: The Vacuum Flask (Thermos)

  • Double-walled vacuum gap: Prevents conduction and convection (both require a physical medium of particles).
  • Silvery shiny inner walls: Reflects infrared radiation back into hot liquids, minimising radiative heat loss.
  • Plastic / Cork stopper: Good insulator stopping conduction and prevents warm vapour from escaping (stopping convection and evaporation).
