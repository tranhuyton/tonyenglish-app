⚡ 1. Electrical Quantities & Ohm's Law (P4.1)

Electric Charge (Q) & Current (I)

I = Q / t ⇔ Q = I × t

Charge Q is in Coulombs (C). Current I is in Amperes (A). Current is the rate of flow of charge (1 A = 1 C/s).

Potential Difference (V) & e.m.f.

V = W / Q (1 Volt = 1 Joule / Coulomb)

Energy transferred per unit charge passed. e.m.f. is supplied by a source (battery), while p.d. is dropped across a load (resistor).

Relationships between Potential Difference (V), Current (I), Resistance (R), and Power (P).

Ohm's Law (V = I × R)

• V (Potential Difference): Measured in Volts (V) using a voltmeter in parallel.
• I (Current): Measured in Amperes (A) using an ammeter in series (I = Q / t).
• R (Resistance): Opposition to current flow, measured in Ohms (Ω). R = V / I.
• Ohmic Conductor: Current is directly proportional to p.d. at constant temperature (straight line through origin).

Electrical Power (P = I × V)

• P (Power): Rate of electrical energy transfer, measured in Watts (W).
• Combining with Ohm's Law: P = I × V = I²R = V² / R.
• Electrical Energy: E = P × t = I × V × t (measured in Joules).

Series Circuit Rules

  • Current: Same at all points (I = I₁ = I₂). One single loop.
  • Voltage: Shared across components (Vtotal = V₁ + V₂).
  • Resistance: Simply sums up: R = R₁ + R₂. Adding resistors increases total resistance.

Parallel Circuit Rules

  • Current: Splits at junctions: Itotal = I₁ + I₂.
  • Voltage: Same across each branch: V = V₁ = V₂.
  • Resistance: 1 / R = 1 / R₁ + 1 / R₂. Combined resistance is ALWAYS less than the smallest individual resistor!

💡 Interactive Potential Divider (Sensors & Dividers)

Hover over environmental conditions to see how Light-Dependent Resistors (LDRs) and Thermistors change resistance and output voltage.

Hover over condition buttons 👆

Potential divider equation: V_out = V_in × (R₂ / (R₁ + R₂)). The component with higher resistance takes a larger share of voltage.

☀️ LDR in Bright Light

Light intensity increases ⇒ LDR Resistance DROPS dramatically (LURD: Light Up, Resistance Down).
Since R_LDR is small, voltage across LDR (V_out) drops close to zero.

🌙 LDR in Darkness

Light intensity decreases ⇒ LDR Resistance RISES very high.
LDR takes a large share of the supply voltage ⇒ V_out RISES (triggers automatic night lights).

🔥 Thermistor in High Temperature

Temperature increases ⇒ NTC Thermistor Resistance DROPS (TURD: Temperature Up, Resistance Down).
Voltage across thermistor drops; voltage across fixed resistor rises (can switch on cooling fan).

❄️ Thermistor in Low Temperature

Temperature decreases ⇒ Thermistor Resistance RISES very high.
Voltage across thermistor rises (can activate fire alarms / frost warning indicators).

🛡️ Electrical Safety in Domestic Appliances

Earth Wire (Green/Yellow):
Provides a low resistance safety path directly to the ground. If a live wire touches the metal casing, huge current flows to earth, blowing the fuse immediately and cutting off the circuit safely.
Fuses & Circuit Breakers:
Connected strictly in the Live wire. Contains a thin wire designed to melt when current exceeds its rating (e.g. 3A, 5A, 13A), breaking the high-voltage supply.
Double Insulation:
Appliance has an all-plastic casing and non-conductive internal chassis. Requires no earth wire (symbol: two concentric squares).

⚡ Electromagnetic Effects: Induction & Motors (P4.3)

🧲 Interactive Induction Lab (Generator Effect)

Hover over Push In and Pull Out to see how an electromotive force (e.m.f.) and current are induced when magnetic field lines are cut.

When a magnet moves relative to a coil, the conductor cuts magnetic field lines, inducing an e.m.f. If circuit is complete, an induced current flows.

➡️ Pushing N-Pole In

• Galvanometer needle deflects to the RIGHT.
• Lenz's Law: The induced current creates an opposing magnetic field (a North pole at the coil entry) to repel the incoming magnet.
• Factors increasing induced e.m.f.: Faster magnet motion, stronger magnet, more turns on coil.

⬅️ Pulling N-Pole Out

• Galvanometer needle deflects in the OPPOSITE direction (LEFT).
• Coil end becomes a South pole to attract and oppose the withdrawing magnet.
• If magnet is held stationary inside the coil: ZERO e.m.f. induced!

🖐️ Fleming's Left-Hand Rule (The Motor Effect)

Used to predict the direction of force on a current-carrying conductor placed in an external magnetic field:
• Thumb: Direction of Motion / Force (F)
• First Finger: Magnetic Field direction (N to S)
• Second Finger: Conventional Current direction (+ to −)

🏭 Transformers & High-Voltage Transmission

Transformer Equation:
Vp / Vs = Np / Ns
• Step-up Transformer: Ns > Np ⇒ Vs > Vp.
• Step-down Transformer: Ns < Np ⇒ Vs < Vp.
• 100% Efficiency: IpVp = IsVs.
Why High Voltage in the National Grid?
• Cables have electrical resistance R.
• Power loss as heat: Ploss = I²R.
• Stepping up voltage V reduces transmission current I enormously.
• Since power loss is proportional to I², reducing current dramatically minimises energy wasted as heat in grid lines.
