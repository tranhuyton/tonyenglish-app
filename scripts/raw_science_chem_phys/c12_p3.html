Experimental techniques and chemical analysis

C12.1 Experimental design & C12.2 Acid-base titrations

🟦 CORE

  • 1. Name appropriate apparatus for the measurement of time, temperature, mass and volume, including: stop-watches, thermometers, balances, burettes, volumetric pipettes, measuring cylinders, gas syringes.[cite: 3174]
  • 2. Describe a solvent, solute, solution, saturated solution, residue, and filtrate.[cite: 3174]
  • 3. Describe an acid-base titration to include the use of a burette, volumetric pipette, and suitable indicator.[cite: 3174]
  • 4. Describe how to identify the end-point of a titration using an indicator.[cite: 3174]

C12.3 Chromatography & C12.4 Separation and purification

🟦 CORE

  • 1. Describe how paper chromatography is used to separate mixtures of soluble coloured substances, using a suitable solvent. Interpret simple chromatograms.[cite: 3184]
  • 2. Describe and explain methods of separation and purification using: a suitable solvent, filtration, crystallisation, simple distillation, and fractional distillation.[cite: 3192]
  • 3. Suggest suitable separation and purification techniques. Identify substances and assess their purity from melting point and boiling point information.[cite: 3201]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • 4. State and use the equation for Rf value (distance travelled by substance / distance travelled by solvent).[cite: 3204]

C12.5 Identification of ions and gases

🟦 CORE

  • 1. Describe tests to identify the anions: carbonate, chloride, bromide, iodide, nitrate, and sulfate.[cite: 3218]
  • 2. Describe tests using aqueous sodium hydroxide and aqueous ammonia to identify the aqueous cations: ammonium, calcium, copper(II), iron(II), iron(III), and zinc.[cite: 3223]
  • 3. Describe tests to identify the gases: ammonia, carbon dioxide, chlorine, hydrogen, and oxygen.[cite: 3232]
  • 4. Describe the use of a flame test to identify the cations: lithium, sodium, potassium, and copper(II).[cite: 3238]
