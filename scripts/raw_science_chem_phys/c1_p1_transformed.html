1. The Three States of Matter & Particle Structure

The Kinetic Particle Theory states that all matter is made up of tiny moving particles (atoms, molecules, or ions). The physical properties of a substance depend on particle arrangement, separation, and movement.

🎧 Nghe mục này

🧊 Solid

  • Arrangement: Regular repeating lattice structure.
  • Separation: Tightly packed, touching each other.
  • Motion: Vibrate only about fixed positions.
  • Forces: Very strong forces of attraction.
  • Properties: Fixed shape and fixed volume; incompressible; high density.
🎧 Nghe mục này

💧 Liquid

  • Arrangement: Randomly arranged, no regular pattern.
  • Separation: Closely packed, most particles touching.
  • Motion: Slide and roll past one another freely.
  • Forces: Moderate forces of attraction.
  • Properties: Fixed volume, takes shape of container; cannot be compressed.
🎧 Nghe mục này

💨 Gas

  • Arrangement: Completely random arrangement.
  • Separation: Very far apart with large empty spaces.
  • Motion: Move rapidly and randomly in all directions.
  • Forces: Negligible / extremely weak attractive forces.
  • Properties: No fixed shape or volume; easily compressed; low density.
Property Solid Liquid Gas
Shape Definite / Fixed Takes container shape Fills entire container
Volume Fixed Fixed Variable
Compressibility Incompressible Virtually incompressible Easily compressed

2. Changes of State & Heating Curves

Changes of state are physical changes (reversible; no new chemical bonds are made). Particles absorb or release thermal energy, altering their kinetic energy and overcoming attractive forces.

🎧 Nghe mục này
⚠️ Critical Exam Trap: Boiling vs. Evaporation
Feature Boiling Evaporation
Temperature Occurs at a precise, fixed boiling point. Occurs at any temperature below boiling point.
Location Throughout the entire liquid (bubbles form). Only at the surface of the liquid.
Rate & Energy Rapid; requires continuous external heat source. Slow; high-energy particles escape, causing cooling effect.

Heating Curves & Temperature Plateaus

Why does temperature stay constant during melting and boiling?
The thermal energy absorbed is used to overcome intermolecular attractive forces between particles, rather than increasing their kinetic energy.

3. Effects of Temperature & Pressure on Gas Volume

🎧 Nghe mục này

🌡️ Effect of Temperature

  • When temperature rises, particles gain kinetic energy and move faster.
  • They strike container walls more frequently and with greater force.
  • Volume increases if pressure remains constant: V ∝ T.
🎧 Nghe mục này

🗜️ Effect of Pressure

  • Increasing external pressure pushes particles closer together.
  • Empty spaces between gas particles decrease.
  • Volume decreases at constant temperature: V ∝ 1/P.

4. Diffusion & Effect of Relative Molecular Mass

🎧 Nghe mục này

Definition (Cambridge Syllabus):
Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of their higher concentration to a region of their lower concentration down a concentration gradient, as a result of their random movement.

Factors Affecting Diffusion Rate:

  • 1. Temperature: Higher temperatures give particles greater kinetic energy, causing faster diffusion.
  • 2. Relative Molecular Mass (Mᵣ): Gases with lower Mᵣ (lighter particles) travel faster and diffuse more rapidly than heavier gases.
🎧 Nghe mục này

🔬 Investigation: Ammonia (NH₃) & Hydrogen Chloride (HCl) Diffusion

Key Reaction: NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s)

Why does the white ring form closer to the HCl end?
1. Ammonia (NH₃) has a lower relative molecular mass (Mᵣ = 14 + 3 = 17) than hydrogen chloride (HCl, Mᵣ = 1 + 35.5 = 36.5).
2. Therefore, NH₃ molecules have a higher average speed and diffuse faster than HCl molecules.
3. NH₃ travels further along the tube in the same time, meeting HCl closer to the hydrochloric acid end.
