Nuclear physics

P5.1 The nucleus

🟦 CORE

  • 1. Describe the composition of the nucleus in terms of protons and neutrons.[cite: 1518]
  • 2. State the relative charges of protons, neutrons and electrons as +1, 0 and -1 respectively.[cite: 1518]
  • 3. Define the terms proton number (atomic number) Z and nucleon number (mass number) A and be able to calculate the number of neutrons in a nucleus.[cite: 1518]
  • 4. Use the nuclide notation AZX.[cite: 1518]
  • 5. State that an element may have more than one isotope and know that some isotopes are radioactive.[cite: 1518]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • 6. Know the relationship between the proton number and the relative charge on a nucleus.[cite: 1518]
  • 7. Describe the processes of nuclear fission and nuclear fusion as the splitting and joining of nuclei.[cite: 1518]

P5.2 Radioactivity

🟦 CORE

  • P5.2.1 Detection:
  • 1. Know what is meant by ionising nuclear radiation and background radiation.[cite: 1518]
  • 2. Know the sources of background radiation: radon gas, rocks, food, cosmic rays.[cite: 1518]
  • 3. Know radiation can be measured using a detector connected to a counter.[cite: 1518]
  • 4. Use count rate measured in counts/s or counts/minute.[cite: 1518]
  • P5.2.2 The three types of nuclear emission:
  • 1. Identify alpha (α), beta (β) and gamma (γ) emissions by their nature, relative ionising effects and penetrating abilities.[cite: 1518]
  • P5.2.3 Radioactive decay:
  • 1. Know that radioactive decay is a spontaneous and random change in an unstable nucleus emitting α, β, or γ.[cite: 1518]
  • 2. Know that during α-decay or β-decay, the nucleus changes to that of a different element.[cite: 1518]
  • 3. Know the change during β-emission: neutron → proton + electron.[cite: 1518]
  • P5.2.4 Half-life:
  • 1. Define the half-life of a particular isotope as the time taken for half the nuclei to decay; use this in simple calculations.[cite: 1518]
  • P5.2.5 Applications and safety:
  • 1. Know applications: smoke alarms, irradiating food, sterilisation, measuring thicknesses, cancer diagnosis/treatment.[cite: 1518]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • P5.2.3 Radioactive decay:
  • 4. Use decay equations, using nuclide notation, to show the emission of α-particles, β-particles and γ-radiation.[cite: 1518]
  • P5.2.5 Applications and safety precautions:
  • 2. State the effects of ionising nuclear radiation on living things, including cell death, mutations and cancer.[cite: 1518]
  • 3. Describe how radioactive materials are moved, used and stored safely in terms of time, distance and shielding.[cite: 1518]
