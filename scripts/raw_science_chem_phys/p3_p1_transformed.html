🌊 1. General Wave Properties (P3.1)

🌊 Interactive Wave Visualizer

Hover over Transverse and Longitudinal diagrams below to inspect particle vibration direction versus wave travel direction.

Hover over wave types 👆

Waves transfer energy without transferring matter. Distinguish between Transverse and Longitudinal waves based on particle vibration direction.

🌊 Transverse Waves (S-waves, Light, Water)

Definition: The direction of particle vibration (oscillation) is perpendicular (at 90°) to the direction of energy propagation.

  • Features: Peaks (Crests) and Troughs.
  • Required Examples: Electromagnetic waves (Light, Radio, X-rays), Water waves, Seismic S-waves.

🔊 Longitudinal Waves (Sound, P-waves)

Definition: The direction of particle vibration (oscillation) is parallel to the direction of energy propagation.

  • Compressions: Regions of high particle density and high pressure.
  • Rarefactions: Regions of low particle density and low pressure.
  • Required Examples: Sound waves, Ultrasound, Seismic P-waves.

Essential Wave Parameters

Parameter Symbol SI Unit Rigorous Cambridge Definition
Amplitude A metres (m) The maximum displacement of a particle from its undisturbed equilibrium position.
Wavelength λ metres (m) The distance between two consecutive identical points on a wave (e.g. crest to crest).
Frequency f Hertz (Hz) The number of complete waves passing a point per unit time (per second).
Period T seconds (s) The time taken for one complete wave to pass a point: T = 1 / f.

🧮 2. The Wave Equation & Wave Behaviors

v = f × λ

The Universal Wave Equation

• v: Wave speed in metres per second (m/s)
• f: Frequency in Hertz (Hz = 1/s)
• λ: Wavelength in metres (m)
• Period Relation: T = 1 / f ⇔ f = 1 / T

🪞 Reflection

Law of Reflection: Angle of incidence (i) = Angle of reflection (r). Both angles are measured strictly to the normal line (90°).
• Wave speed v, frequency f, and wavelength λ are strictly unchanged.

💧 Refraction

Change of wave speed when entering a medium of different optical density.
• Frequency f is strictly constant.
• Into denser medium: Speed v slows down, wavelength λ shortens, ray bends towards the normal.

🌊 Diffraction

The spreading out of waves as they pass through a narrow gap or around an obstacle.
• Maximum Diffraction: Occurs when gap width is approximately equal to wavelength (gap width ≈ λ).

👁️ 3. Light, Lenses & Dispersion

🔍 Converging (Convex) Lens Ray Diagram

  • Principal Focus (F): The point where rays parallel to the principal axis converge after refraction.
  • Focal Length (f): Distance between optical centre and principal focus.
  • Real vs Virtual: Real image formed when light rays actually meet on a screen (inverted). Virtual image cannot be projected onto a screen (magnifying glass, upright).

🌈 Dispersion of White Light

  • White light is a mixture of 7 spectral colours: Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet (ROYGBIV).
  • Red light: Lowest frequency, longest λ, refracts and deviates least.
  • Violet light: Highest frequency, shortest λ, slows down most in glass, refracts and deviates most.

📐 Supplement: Refractive Index & Total Internal Reflection

Refractive Index (n):
n = sin i / sin r
Ratio of speed in vacuum to speed in medium: n = c / v.
Critical Angle (c):
sin c = 1 / n
Angle of incidence in denser medium where angle of refraction is 90°.
Total Internal Reflection (TIR):
Occurs only when light travels from denser to less dense medium AND angle of incidence i > critical angle c. Used in optical fibers.

All electromagnetic waves are transverse, travel at the speed of light c ≈ 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum, and can travel through space.

📡 Radio Waves

• Properties: Longest wavelength, lowest frequency & photon energy.
• Uses: Radio and television broadcasts, RFID communication, Bluetooth.
• Safety: Non-ionising radiation; completely harmless at everyday exposure levels.

📱 Microwaves

• Uses: Satellite communication (penetrates Earth's ionosphere), mobile phones, microwave ovens (heats water molecules in food).
• Hazards: Internal heating of body cells and tissue.

🌡️ Infrared (IR)

• Uses: TV remote controls, thermal imaging cameras, night vision equipment, radiant electric heaters.
• Hazards: Skin burns from high-intensity infrared radiation.

👁️ Visible Light

• Range: Red (longest λ ~700 nm) to Violet (shortest λ ~400 nm).
• Uses: Vision, photography, fiber optic communication (total internal reflection).

☀️ Ultraviolet (UV)

• Uses: Fluorescent lamps, sterilising medical equipment, bank note fraud detection.
• Hazards: Sunburn, accelerated skin aging, cataracts, risk of skin cancer (ionising).

🦴 X-Rays

• Uses: Medical diagnostic imaging of broken bones, airport luggage security scanners.
• Hazards: Highly ionising radiation; penetrates soft tissue, damages DNA causing cell mutations.

☢️ Gamma Rays

• Uses: Radiotherapy (destroying cancerous tumors), sterilising surgical tools and food packaging.
• Hazards: Highest penetrating power, extremely ionising, causes severe cell death and cancer.

🔊 5. Sound Waves & Echoes (P3.4)

Pitch & Frequency

• Pitch depends on Frequency: Higher frequency (more wave crests per second) ⇒ higher pitched sound.
• Human Hearing Range: Approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz). Ultrasound is any sound above 20 kHz.

Loudness & Amplitude

• Loudness depends on Amplitude: Greater amplitude (taller wave crests) ⇒ louder sound carrying greater acoustic energy.
• Sound requires a Medium: Sound travels fastest in Solids (~5000 m/s), slower in Liquids (~1500 m/s), slowest in Air (~330-340 m/s), cannot travel in a Vacuum.

🚨 CAMBRIDGE EXAM PITFALL: Echo Calculations (Two-Way Journey!)

A very frequent Cambridge trap: An echo is the reflection of sound. The sound wave travels to the obstacle AND returns back to the listener. Therefore, Total Distance Travelled = 2 × d!
• Speed of sound: v = 2d / t ⇔ Distance to cliff: d = (v × t) / 2.
