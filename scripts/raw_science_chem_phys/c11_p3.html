Organic chemistry

C11.1 Formulas and terminology

🟦 CORE

  • 1. Draw and interpret the displayed formula of a molecule to show all the atoms and all the bonds.[cite: 3063]
  • 2. State that a saturated compound has molecules in which all carbon-carbon bonds are single bonds.[cite: 3066]
  • 3. State that an unsaturated compound has molecules in which one or more carbon-carbon bonds are not single bonds.[cite: 3069]
  • 4. State that a homologous series is a family of similar compounds with similar chemical properties.[cite: 3083]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • 5. Describe the general characteristics of a homologous series as: (a) having the same general formula (b) displaying a trend in physical properties.[cite: 3084]

C11.2 Naming organic compounds

🟦 CORE

  • 1. Name and draw the displayed formulas of: (a) methane and ethane (b) ethene (c) ethanol.[cite: 3075]
  • 2. State the type of compound present, given a chemical name ending in -ane, -ene or -ol, or from a molecular formula or displayed formula.[cite: 3080]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • 3. Name and draw the structural formulas and displayed formulas of unbranched: (a) alkanes (b) alkenes, including but-1-ene and but-2-ene (not cis/trans) containing up to four carbon atoms per molecule.[cite: 3088]

C11.3 Fuels

🟦 CORE

  • 1. Name the fossil fuels: coal, natural gas and petroleum.[cite: 3103]
  • 2. Name methane as the main constituent of natural gas.[cite: 3104]
  • 3. State that hydrocarbons are compounds that contain hydrogen and carbon only.[cite: 3105]
  • 4. State that petroleum is a mixture of hydrocarbons.[cite: 3106]
  • 5. Describe the separation of petroleum into useful fractions by fractional distillation.[cite: 3107]
  • 6. Name the uses of the fractions as: refinery gas, gasoline/petrol, naphtha, diesel oil/gas oil, bitumen.[cite: 3108]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • 7. Describe how the properties of fractions obtained from petroleum change from the bottom to the top of the fractionating column, limited to: (a) decreasing chain length (b) lower boiling points.[cite: 3116]

C11.4 Alkanes, C11.5 Alkenes, C11.6 Alcohols & C11.7 Polymers

🟦 CORE

  • Alkanes: Describe properties as being generally unreactive, except in terms of combustion.[cite: 3125]
  • Alkenes: Describe the test to distinguish between saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons by their reaction with aqueous bromine.[cite: 3135]
  • Alcohols: Describe the complete combustion of ethanol. State the uses of ethanol as a solvent and a fuel.[cite: 3140]
  • Polymers: Define polymers as large molecules built up from many smaller molecules called monomers. Describe the formation of poly(ethene).[cite: 3148]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • Alkenes: Describe the manufacture of alkenes and hydrogen by the cracking of larger alkane molecules. Describe addition reactions with bromine, hydrogen and steam.[cite: 3153]
  • Polymers: Identify repeat units. Describe differences between addition and condensation polymerisation. Describe and draw the structure of nylon (a polyamide).[cite: 3167]
