Electricity and magnetism

P4.1 Simple phenomena of magnetism

🟦 CORE

  • 1. Describe the forces between magnetic poles and between magnets and magnetic materials, including attraction and repulsion, magnetised and unmagnetised.[cite: 1518]
  • 2. State the differences between the properties of temporary magnets (made of soft iron) and permanent magnets (made of steel).[cite: 1518]
  • 3. State the difference between magnetic and non-magnetic materials.[cite: 1518]
  • 4. Describe how a permanent magnet differs from an electromagnet.[cite: 1518]

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • 5. Describe a magnetic field as a region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force.[cite: 1518]
  • 6. State that the direction of a magnetic field at a point is the direction of the force on the N pole of a magnet at that point.[cite: 1518]
  • 7. Describe induced magnetism.[cite: 1518]

P4.2 Electrical quantities

🟦 CORE

  • P4.2.1 Electrical charge:
  • 1. State that there are positive and negative charges.
  • 2. State that positive charges repel other positive charges, negative charges repel other negative charges, but positive charges attract negative charges.
  • 3. Describe electrostatic charging by friction, and simple methods to determine if an object is charged.
  • 4. Distinguish between electrical conductors and insulators and give typical examples.
  • P4.2.2 Electric current:
  • 1. Know that electric current is related to the flow of charge.
  • 2. Know that electric current in metals is related to the flow of electrons.
  • 3. Describe the use of ammeters (analogue and digital) with different ranges.
  • 4. Know the difference between direct current (d.c.) and alternating current (a.c.).
  • P4.2.3 Voltage:
  • 1. Describe the voltage of the source as the cause of current in the circuit.
  • 2. Know that the voltage of the source is shared between the components in a series circuit.
  • 3. Describe the use of voltmeters (analogue and digital).
  • P4.2.4 Resistance:
  • 1. Recall and use the equation R = V / I.
  • 2. Describe an experiment to determine resistance using a voltmeter and an ammeter and do the appropriate calculations.
  • P4.2.5 Electrical energy and power:
  • 1. Understand that electric circuits transfer energy from a source of electrical energy to the circuit components and then into the surroundings.
  • 2. Recall and use the equation P = IV.
  • 3. Recall and use the equation E = IVt.
  • 4. Define the kilowatt-hour (kWh) and calculate the cost of using electrical appliances.

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • P4.2.1 Electrical charge:
  • 5. Know that charging of solids by friction involves only a transfer of negative charge (electrons).
  • 6. State that charge is measured in coulombs.
  • 7. Describe an electric field as a region in which an electric charge experiences a force.
  • 8. State that the direction of an electric field at a point is the direction of the force on a positive charge at that point.
  • P4.2.2 Electric current:
  • 5. Define electric current as the charge passing a point per unit time; recall and use the equation I = Q / t.
  • 6. Describe electrical conduction in metals in terms of the movement of delocalised (mobile) electrons.
  • 7. State that conventional current is from positive to negative and that the flow of electrons is from negative to positive.
  • P4.2.3 Voltage:
  • 4. Define electromotive force (e.m.f.) as the electrical work done by a source in moving a unit charge around a complete circuit.
  • 5. Know that e.m.f. is measured in volts (V).
  • 6. Define potential difference (p.d.) as the work done by a unit charge passing between two points in a circuit.
  • 7. Know that p.d. is measured in volts (V).
  • P4.2.4 Resistance:
  • 3. Sketch and explain the current-voltage graph of a resistor of constant resistance.
  • 4. Recall and use the relationship: (a) resistance is directly proportional to length (b) resistance is inversely proportional to cross-sectional area.

P4.3 Electrical circuits & P4.4 Electrical safety

🟦 CORE

  • P4.3.1 Circuit components:
  • 1. Draw and interpret circuit diagrams containing cells, batteries, power supplies, switches, resistors, heaters, lamps, motors, ammeters, voltmeters and fuses.
  • P4.3.2 Series and parallel circuits:
  • 1. Know that the current at every point in a series circuit is the same.
  • 2. Know how to construct and use series and parallel circuits.
  • 3. Calculate the combined resistance of two or more resistors in series.
  • 4. Know the advantages of connecting lamps in parallel.
  • 5. Know that, for a parallel circuit, the current from the source is larger than the current in each branch.
  • P4.4 Electrical safety:
  • 1. Describe the heating effect of current.
  • 2. State the hazards of damaged insulation, overheating cables, damp conditions, excess current.
  • 3. Explain the use and operation of trip switches and fuses.
  • 4. Explain why the outer casing of an electrical appliance must be non-conducting (double-insulated) or earthed.

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • P4.3.1 Circuit components:
  • 2. Draw and interpret circuit diagrams containing generators and light-emitting diodes (LEDs).
  • P4.3.2 Series and parallel circuits:
  • 6. Know that the combined resistance of two resistors in parallel is less than that of either resistor by itself.
  • 7. Recall and use in calculations: (a) sum of currents entering a junction = sum leaving (b) total p.d. in series = sum of individual p.d.s (c) p.d. across each branch of a parallel arrangement is the p.d. across the whole arrangement.
  • 8. Calculate the combined resistance of two resistors in parallel.

P4.5 Electromagnetic effects

🟦 CORE

(No core learning outcomes for this topic)

🟩 SUPPLEMENT

  • P4.5.1 Electromagnetic induction:
    1. Know that a conductor moving across a magnetic field or a changing magnetic field linking with a conductor can induce an e.m.f.
    2. State the factors affecting the magnitude of an induced e.m.f.
  • P4.5.2 The a.c. generator:
    1. Describe a simple a.c. generator (rotating coil, slip rings, brushes).
    2. Sketch and interpret graphs of e.m.f. against time.
  • P4.5.3 Magnetic effect of current:
    1. Describe the pattern and direction of the magnetic field due to currents in straight wires and in solenoids.
    2. Describe the effect of changing the magnitude and direction of the current.
  • P4.5.4 Force on a current-carrying conductor:
    1. Know that a force acts on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field, including the effect of reversing current or field.
    2. Recall and use the relative directions of force, magnetic field and current.
  • P4.5.5 The d.c. motor:
    1. Know that a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field may experience a turning effect; increased by more turns, current, field strength.
    2. Describe the operation of an electric motor (split-ring commutator and brushes).
  • P4.5.6 The transformer:
    1. Describe the construction of a basic transformer with a soft-iron core.
    2. Use the terms primary, secondary, step-up and step-down.
    3. Recall and use Vp / Vs = Np / Ns.
    4. Recall and use IpVp = IsVs for 100% efficiency.
    5. Describe the use of transformers in high-voltage transmission.
    6. Recall and use P = I²R to explain power loss reduction.
